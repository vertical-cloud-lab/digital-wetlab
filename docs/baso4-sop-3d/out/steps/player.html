1. Where each step happens

1.1 · The two rooms

Where: overview · 13 s

Two rooms in two buildings. The stocks are made in EB B125, Chris Dillon's lab, because it has the fume hood. The plate is filled and watched on the OT-2 in CB 154, under the lid camera.

1.2 · Inside the hood: barium chloride only

Where: overview · 16 s

Inside the hood (red): the barium chloride bottle is only ever opened here, and every barium solution stays here until its tube is capped. Stand outside it with only your arms in, the sash at its working-height sticker.

download 1-02_inside-the-hood.mp4

1.3 · Next to the hood: balance and the other salts

Where: overview · 18 s

Next to the hood (amber), on the bench: the A&D HR-100A balance, sodium sulfate and sodium chloride (neither is classed as hazardous, so they are weighed on the bench), labels and the log. Barium chloride only reaches the balance inside a capped vial.

download 1-03_next-to-the-hood.mp4

1.4 · Everything travels capped, in the tub

Where: overview · 10 s

Everything moves between the buildings capped, in the lidded tub: the two tube racks with their ten tubes. Nothing is opened on the way.

download 1-04_carry-in-the-tub.mp4

1.5 · CB 154: uncap only on the OT-2's deck

Where: overview · 16 s

In CB 154 (blue): the tubes are uncapped only on the OT-2's deck. The plate sits in slot 7 on black paper, under the lid camera, and is sealed at the protocol's pause. The run, the stills and the end of the run all happen here.

download 1-05_cb154-ot2.mp4

1.6 · The order of the day

Where: overview · 13 s

The order: make the stocks in EB B125, carry them over, load and fill the plate on the OT-2, then let the lid camera record it for 72 h. The next three chapters go through each part.

download 1-06_the-order.mp4

2a. Barium chloride, inside the hood

2.1 · PPE and the SDS

Where: EB B125 · 15 s

Before anything is opened: lab coat, splash goggles and nitrile gloves (two pairs for barium). The barium chloride SDS goes in the binder. Chris Dillon's rule for the room: keep every container closed and labelled.

download 2-01_ppe-and-sds.mp4

2.2 · Set up and check the balance

Where: EB B125, next to the hood · 17 s

The balance sits on the bench beside the hood. If it was carried over, plug it in at least an hour before weighing: the HR-100A drifts for about an hour after a move (powder-doser 157). Level it by its bubble and check it with a check weight.

download 2-02_balance-check.mp4

2.3 · Load the hood, sash down

Where: EB B125, inside the hood · 20 s

Load the hood before opening anything: absorbent liner, a tray, the barium waste bottle, a wash bottle of fresh DI water, the barium chloride, a tube rack and a spatula, all behind the yellow line 15 cm (6 in) inside the sash. Then lower the sash to its working-height sticker.

download 2-03_load-the-hood.mp4

2.4 · Tare the capped vial

Where: EB B125, next to the hood · 16 s

At the balance: lift the breeze-break lid, put the empty capped 20 mL vial on the pan, close the lid and press TARE. A capped vial is how barium chloride reaches the balance: the powder never leaves the hood in an open container.

download 2-04_tare-the-vial.mp4

2.5 · Carry the vial into the hood

Where: next to, then inside the hood · 9 s

Walk the tared, capped vial from the balance into the hood. Only your arms go in; your head stays outside the sash.

download 2-05_vial-into-hood.mp4

2.6 · Uncap the barium chloride and the vial

Where: EB B125, inside the hood · 9 s

Inside the hood only: unscrew the barium chloride bottle and the vial, and set both caps down top-down on the liner.

download 2-06_uncap-bottle-and-vial.mp4

2.7 · Spatula about 366 mg of barium chloride

Where: EB B125, inside the hood · 13 s

Spatula about 366 mg of barium chloride dihydrate into the vial, low in the hood and slowly, so no dust lifts. It doesn't have to be exact: the balance reading sets the concentration.

download 2-07_spatula-barium-chloride.mp4

2.8 · Cap both, wipe the vial

Where: EB B125, inside the hood · 10 s

Cap the bottle and the vial before anything leaves the hood. Wipe the vial's outside with a damp wipe; the wipe goes in the barium waste.

download 2-08_cap-and-wipe.mp4

2.9 · Weigh the capped vial and log it

Where: EB B125, next to the hood · 16 s

Back at the balance: weigh the capped vial and write the reading in the log, with the bottle's lot. 0.3664 g in 15.00 mL is 0.1000 M (244.26 g/mol); for another mass, work out its concentration and use that in the plate map.

download 2-09_weigh-the-vial.mp4

2b. Dilutions, then the bench

2.10 · Dissolve it and pour into the 0.1 M tube

Where: EB B125, inside the hood · 14 s

In the hood: about 10 mL of DI water into the vial, cap and swirl until clear, then pour it into the tube marked 0.1 M barium chloride. Uncap the four barium tubes now and set their caps down top-down.

download 2-10_dissolve-barium-chloride.mp4

2.11 · Rinse, make up to 15 mL, invert

Where: EB B125, inside the hood · 12 s

Rinse the vial twice into the tube, make it up to the 15 mL mark with DI water, cap it and invert it ten times. The empty vial is rinsed into the barium waste.

download 2-11_make-up-barium-0.1M.mp4

2.12 · Dilute to 10, 1 and 0.1 mM barium chloride

Where: EB B125, inside the hood · 15 s

Still in the hood: 13.5 mL of DI water in each of the next three tubes, then 1.5 mL down the series with a fresh pipette tip each time: 10 mM, 1 mM and 0.1 mM barium chloride. Mix each before the next transfer.

download 2-12_barium-dilutions.mp4

2.13 · Cap and label the barium tubes

Where: EB B125, inside the hood · 12 s

Cap all four barium tubes. Label each one: name, concentration, date, initials. Pipette tips and wipes that touched barium go in the barium waste in the hood.

download 2-13_cap-and-label-barium.mp4

2.14 · Weigh about 213 mg of sodium sulfate

Where: EB B125, next to the hood · 16 s

Sodium sulfate is weighed on the bench, in an open weigh boat: tare the boat, open the bottle, spatula about 213 mg into the boat, close the bottle. 0.2131 g of anhydrous sodium sulfate (142.04 g/mol) in 15.00 mL is 0.1000 M.

download 2-14_weigh-sodium-sulfate.mp4

2.15 · Make up the 0.1 M sodium sulfate

Where: EB B125, next to the hood · 11 s

Tip the boat through a funnel into the 0.1 M sodium sulfate tube, rinse the boat and funnel in with DI water, and make it up to 15 mL. Cap and invert.

download 2-15_make-up-sodium-sulfate.mp4

2.16 · Sulfate dilutions, 5 M NaCl and DI water

Where: EB B125, next to the hood · 14 s

The same tenfold series for 10, 1 and 0.1 mM sodium sulfate. Then 4.383 g of sodium chloride made up to 15 mL (5 M), and a tube of fresh DI water: ten tubes in all, each labelled and logged.

download 2-16_sulfate-nacl-water.mp4

2.17 · Pack the tub and clean up the hood

Where: EB B125, hood then bench · 18 s

Wipe the outside of the barium tubes and the rack in the hood, then carry the rack straight to the tub; the bench rack joins it. Lid on. Outer gloves off in the hood, liner and wipes into the barium waste, sash fully down. Leave the hood as you found it.

download 2-17_pack-and-clean-up.mp4

Before the day: the OT-2 calibrated (deck, tip length, pipette offset, then Labware Position Check), the Pi 5 on its 27 W supply, the camera's exposure and white balance locked, the rail lights on with no off timer, and the capture job tested.

Where: CB 154, OT-2 deck · 11 s

Open the OT-2's door. The colour sensor stays parked in slot 10, and nothing else drives the robot while the protocol runs (one controller at a time).

Where: CB 154, OT-2 deck · 12 s

Slot 7, under the lid camera: black paper, then the NEST 96-well plate with A1 at the back left. Take its lid off only now. The yellow cone is what the camera sees.

download 3-03_plate-in-slot-7.mp4

3.4 · Tips in slot 6

Where: CB 154, OT-2 deck · 7 s

A full rack of Opentrons 300 uL tips in slot 6 (the P300 is the only pipette). Lid off.

download 3-04_tips-in-slot-6.mp4

3.5 · The racks in slots 4 and 5

Where: CB 154, OT-2 deck · 14 s

Lift the two racks out of the tub and onto the deck as they are: barium chloride in slot 4; sodium sulfate, the 5 M NaCl and the DI water in slot 5, in the positions the protocol's map gives.

download 3-05_racks-in-slots-4-5.mp4

3.6 · Uncap the ten tubes

Where: CB 154, OT-2 deck · 12 s

Uncap the ten tubes one at a time, here on the deck and only now. Each cap goes top-down on a clean wipe, in the same order, so it goes back on its own tube.

3.7 · Start the capture job, then the protocol

Close the door. Start the capture job on the Pi first, so the record covers the filling too, then start the protocol (simulated beforehand with opentrons_simulate).

3b. The fill and the seal

3.8 · Water into 84 wells

Where: CB 154, OT-2, door closed · 12 s

84 wells: DI water first, one tip for the pass, dispensing from above.

download 3-08_fill-water.mp4

3.9 · 5 M NaCl into 64 wells

Where: CB 154, OT-2, door closed · 13 s

64 wells: 40 uL of 5 M NaCl (1 molal in 200 uL). Barium chloride and sodium sulfate come from the 0.1 M, 10, 1 and 0.1 mM tubes: 20 uL gives 1/10 of the stock, 60 uL of 10 mM gives 3 mM.

download 3-09_fill-nacl.mp4

3.10 · Barium chloride into 72 wells

Where: CB 154, OT-2, door closed · 11 s

72 wells: barium chloride, from the tube each well needs (shown: 1 mM). A dose under 20 uL, the P300's minimum, stops the protocol instead of becoming zero.

download 3-10_fill-barium-chloride.mp4

3.11 · Sodium sulfate last, a new tip per well

Where: CB 154, OT-2, door closed · 14 s

72 wells: sodium sulfate last, a new tip for every well, dispensed from above the liquid, so no tip that touched barium goes back into a stock. Each well's start time comes from the run log.

download 3-11_fill-sodium-sulfate-last.mp4

3.12 · Seal the plate at the pause

Where: CB 154, OT-2 deck · 15 s

At the pause: open the door, lay a clear adhesive seal over the plate and press it down well by well, then close the door and resume. Log the time. Over days an open well would lose water and concentrate both salts.

download 3-12_seal-at-the-pause.mp4

3.13 · Recap the tubes, leave the lights on

Where: CB 154, OT-2 deck · 17 s

When the protocol ends: recap each tube from its place on the wipe (leftover stocks go to the barium waste at the end of the run), close the door, and leave the rail lights on. The head parks away from slot 7 so the camera has a clear view.

download 3-13_recap-the-tubes.mp4

4. Watching it for 72 h

4.1 · The lid camera and its stills schedule

Where: CB 154 · 18 s

The lid camera (Raspberry Pi HQ camera, 8-50 mm zoom, Pi 5) looks straight down through the top window at slot 7. The capture job takes a still every minute until 30 min after the last sulfate, every 5 min until 6 h, then every 15 min: about 400 stills in 72 h.

download 4-01_lid-camera-schedule.mp4

4.2 · What the camera sees

Where: CB 154 · 9 s

Its view: about 200 pixels across each well. Clear wells look dark on the black paper; barium sulfate shows as white.

download 4-02_camera-view.mp4

4.3 · The 72 h time-lapse (illustrative)

Where: CB 154 · 16 s

Illustrative timing: wells with the highest saturation index (PHREEQC, 10-30 table) turn white within minutes, those near +1 take hours, and those at or below 0 never should. The real times are what this run measures.

download 4-03_timelapse-illustrative.mp4

4.4 · The daily check

Where: CB 154 · 16 s

Once a day, someone checks the stream and the heartbeat and writes anything that happened in the event log: a door opened, a reboot, a missed still. The Pi's 05:00, 13:00 and 21:00 reboots are held off while the run's lock is on.

download 4-04_daily-check.mp4

4.5 · At 72 h: last still, weigh, photograph

Where: CB 154 · 12 s

At 72 h: take a last still, then take the plate out, weigh it (start and end weights give the water lost) and photograph it on black with a phone, as in March.

download 4-05_end-of-run.mp4

4.6 · Waste and the run record

Where: CB 154 · 18 s

The plate's wells, the leftover stocks and the rinses go in the labelled barium waste; Risk Management collects it. The run record, plate map, well start times, stills index and the environment and event logs go in the repo; the stills go to the agreed storage.

download 4-06_waste-and-run-record.mp4
