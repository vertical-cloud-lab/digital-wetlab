## Justification

Reviewers were selected to align with **Panel 2 (Geochemistry and Biogeochemical Cycling)** and the proposal's focus on determining Pitzer ion-interaction parameters for sulfide-mineral precipitation in petroleum-derived produced-water brines. The selection criteria prioritize: (1) research alignment with aqueous geochemistry, mineral precipitation/dissolution, electrolyte thermodynamics, and geochemical modeling (PHREEQC/Pitzer); (2) familiarity with the ACS PRF review process; and (3) institutional diversity.

1. **Chen Zhu** — AAAS Fellow and Professor of Earth and Atmospheric Sciences at Indiana University. Leading expert in geochemical modeling, water-rock interactions, and aqueous geochemistry. His research on the thermodynamics and kinetics of mineral-water reactions, including diagenesis and CO₂-water-rock interactions, directly parallels the proposal's PHREEQC-based Pitzer parameter regression for sulfide minerals. He has developed geochemical modeling platforms and is co-Executive Editor of *Ground Water*. Among the strongest Pitzer/PHREEQC-aligned reviewers available.

2. **Young-Shin Jun** — Professor of Energy, Environmental and Chemical Engineering at Washington University in St. Louis. Studies mineral nucleation and precipitation at solid-water interfaces, including geologic CO₂ sequestration and resource recovery. Her expertise in mineral formation mechanisms from aqueous solution is directly relevant to the proposal's high-throughput precipitation boundary mapping for FeS and PbS in brines.

3. **Gregory Druschel** — Professor of Earth and Environmental Sciences at IU Indianapolis. Specialist in sulfur geochemistry and microbial-geochemical interactions. His research on aqueous sulfide speciation, sulfur redox cycling, and sulfide mineral formation is directly relevant to the proposal's focus on Fe²⁺–HS⁻ and Pb²⁺–HS⁻ systems and the role of petroleum-derived H₂S in sulfide-scale formation.

4. **Elizabeth Swanner** — Professor in the Department of the Earth, Atmosphere, and Climate at Iowa State University. Studies iron and sulfur biogeochemistry, including pyrite (FeS₂) formation from anoxic pore waters and the behavior of metals and minerals under varying aqueous redox conditions. Her expertise in iron sulfide geochemistry and ferruginous aqueous systems directly connects to the proposal's FeS precipitation experiments and the challenge of controlling Fe²⁺ oxidation state.

5. **Jeffrey Catalano** — Professor of Earth, Environmental, and Planetary Sciences at Washington University in St. Louis. Studies geochemical processes controlling mineralogy and trace metal cycling in aquatic systems, including mineral-water interface reactions and environmental biogeochemistry. His expertise in how mineral surfaces and aqueous speciation govern metal fate is relevant to evaluating the proposal's systematic precipitation mapping and thermodynamic modeling approach.

6. **Yuanzhi Tang** — Georgia Power Professor in the School of Earth and Atmospheric Sciences at Georgia Tech. Founding Director of the Center for Critical Mineral Solutions and Executive Director of the Strategic Energy Institute. Specializes in environmental geochemistry at mineral-water interfaces, with expertise in synchrotron X-ray techniques for characterizing precipitates and minerals. Her work on molecular-scale biogeochemical processes is relevant to the proposal's mineral characterization and the broader significance of metal-sulfide precipitation.

### Additional Reviewers (7–14)

7. **Daniel Giammar** — Walter E. Browne Professor of Environmental Engineering at Washington University in St. Louis. Research focuses on chemical reactions affecting the fate and transport of heavy metals in aquatic systems, including reactions at solid-water interfaces. His work on lead corrosion, arsenic/chromium removal, and geologic carbon sequestration provides expertise in aqueous metal chemistry and mineral precipitation/dissolution relevant to the proposal's Pb²⁺ systems.

8. **Lauren Beckingham** — Associate Professor of Civil and Environmental Engineering at Auburn University. Research on reactive transport, mineral reaction rates in porous media, and water-rock interactions. Her modeling expertise in mineral precipitation/dissolution in subsurface systems (including geologic CO₂ sequestration and enhanced oil recovery) connects to the proposal's PHREEQC-based thermodynamic modeling and Pitzer parameter regression.

9. **Milad Abolhasani** — PRF DNI grantee and pioneer of self-driving labs for petroleum-relevant flow chemistry. His Opentrons-based autonomous experimentation is the closest methodological analog to the proposed high-throughput OT-2 precipitation mapping work.

10. **Fang Liu** — PRF DNI 2022 grantee applying computational molecular design to asphaltene quantum chemistry. Rare combination of computational methods with direct petroleum-relevant molecular systems — connects to the petroleum geochemistry aspects of the proposal.

11. **Burcu Beykal** — PRF DNI 2022 grantee using reinforcement learning for petroleum reservoir optimization. One of the few researchers combining AI/ML methods with petroleum applications under PRF funding, relevant to the proposal's adaptive Bayesian optimization approach.

12. **Junwei Lucas Bao** — PRF DNI 2022 grantee who bridges ML/DFT computational methods with petroleum-relevant catalysis (methane dry reforming). Well-positioned to evaluate both the thermodynamic modeling and the Bayesian optimization methodology.

13. **Gwendolyn Bailey** — PRF 2023 grantee studying copper chalcogenide nanoclusters. Her work in aqueous inorganic chemistry and metal–chalcogenide systems (Cu–S) has relevance to the proposal's Fe–S and Pb–S sulfide chemistry.

14. **Sidney E. Creutz** — PRF 2022 grantee studying iron-carbon multiple bonds and chalcogenide synthesis. Direct relevance to the proposal's iron sulfide chemistry.
