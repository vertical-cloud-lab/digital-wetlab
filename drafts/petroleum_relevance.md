# Petroleum Relevance Statement

> **Limit:** 100 words — entered separately in the ACS PRF online portal.

This project investigates how petroleum-derived compounds—H₂S generated by thermochemical and bacterial sulfate reduction of crude oil, and short-chain organic acids (acetic, propionic) produced by thermal maturation of kerogen—interact with dissolved metal ions to form sulfide mineral scales in concentrated brines. Sulfide scale deposition (FeS, PbS) restricts flow and fouls equipment during petroleum production. Current thermodynamic models lack Pitzer interaction parameters for metal–sulfide and metal–organic acid systems in multi-component brines. This project determines these parameters through systematic high-throughput experiments, delivering validated coefficients which can support petroleum-industry scale prediction tools (ScaleSoftPitzer, OLI Systems, PHREEQC) and broader applications in ion interaction predictions.
