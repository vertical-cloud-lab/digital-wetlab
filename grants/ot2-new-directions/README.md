## Short answer

Three directions stand out:

1. **Ni-based and high-entropy electrocatalyst films for water electrolysis, electrodeposited and
   tested on the OT-2, with durability in the loop**
   ([direction 1](#1-electrodeposited-alloy-catalysts-for-water-electrolysis-with-durability-in-the-loop)).
   It fits the lab best. It is AMPERE-2's open hardware with a new objective, and Topic 15 of the
   FY2027 DoD MURI could have been written for it.
2. **Lithium, magnesium and boron from Great Salt Lake and produced-water brines**
   ([direction 2](#2-lithium-magnesium-and-boron-from-great-salt-lake-and-produced-water-brines)).
   It starts fastest: no new hardware, and it has the most deadlines in the window. It also answers
   Reviewer B, by testing well-parameterised major-ion models against equilibrium data.
3. **Co-precipitated oxide and hydroxide libraries**
   ([direction 3](#3-co-precipitated-oxide-and-hydroxide-libraries)). This is the most purely
   materials-science of the three: it turns the lab's precipitation know-how to synthesis. It pairs
   with direction 1 for the MURI and has an NSF program that takes proposals any time.

Teaching through remote experiments ([direction 6](#6-remote-materials-experiments-for-teaching))
has two January deadlines and costs little to write. Nucleation statistics
([direction 4](#4-nucleation-statistics-and-crystallisation-inhibitors-the-current-setup-re-aimed))
is the quickest dataset but has the fewest funders. Direction 5 needs no proposal.

**Calendar, Nov 2026 – Apr 2027.** Programs the alloy proposals already use (AFOSR YIP, ONR YIP,
DOE Early Career, NSF CMMI AMDO, DURIP) are left out so the two lines of work don't compete.

## What the OT-2 is good for here

**Strengths.**
- Aqueous liquid handling from 1 to 300 µL, eight channels at a time, in plates, vials and tubes.
- A heater-shaker (37–95 °C, no cooling).
- An open Python API and the Opentrons HTTP API, which the lab already drives remotely.
- It costs little to run, so it can sit idle between campaigns, or run a teaching demo, without
  anyone worrying about utilisation.

**What it lacks.**
- It measures nothing itself. Readouts come from:
  - the lab's camera and AS7341 colour sensor;
  - campus instruments: a plate reader and ICP-OES (the shopping list assumes campus access; buying
    a plate reader of its own was discussed in byu-vcl issue 68);
  - a potentiostat (the lab has threads for a Squidstat Plus, byu-vcl issue 26, and an EZstat Pro,
    issue 231 and PR 291).
- There's no fume hood in CB154, so volatile organic solvents, H₂S and hot injections are out. This
  rules out most perovskite, MOF-in-DMF and quantum-dot work, the most crowded OT-2 materials areas
  anyway.
- Plates are open: gases exchange and water evaporates over hours (Reviewer B's H₂S point,
  [review digest](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/digital-wetlab/blob/3d6ba0a/grants/acs-prf/REVIEW_2026.md)).
- Filling a 96-well plate one well at a time takes minutes, which matters for anything kinetic.

**What the PRF review teaches.** Pick the measurement to match the question. A turbidity onset
measures nucleation, not solubility. Each direction below says what is measured and why that answers
its question.

**What the lab already plans elsewhere.** The CubXL gantry is lined up for droplet-cell corrosion
(copper for the ONR YIP, and the lab's own AlSi10Mg) and has a first electrochemistry use-case list
([byu-vcl PR 270](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/byu-vcl/pull/270), now
[digital-alloy-lab issue 177](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/digital-alloy-lab/issues/177)).
The directions here are chosen to give the OT-2 its own work beside the CubXL, not to duplicate it.

## Directions, ranked

Ranked by fit with the lab, impact, and how little the OT-2 has to change. Fit is judged against the
lab's three thrusts (autonomous alloy discovery, autonomous electrochemistry, advanced Bayesian
optimization) and its cloud-lab and frugal-twin themes, from the
[org profile](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/.github/blob/main/profile/README.md).

### 1. Electrodeposited alloy catalysts for water electrolysis, with durability in the loop

- **Question.** Which Ni–Fe(–Co/–Mo) compositions and plating conditions give oxygen- or
  hydrogen-evolution activity that *survives* an accelerated stress test? Most automated catalyst
  searches optimise initial activity. Durability is what limits alkaline and AEM electrolyzers, and
  the lab already runs accelerated stress tests on Ni electrodes (`electrolysis-ast`).
- **What the OT-2 does.** Mixes plating baths from metal-salt stocks, electrodeposits a film on each
  electrode, rinses, and runs CV/LSV/EIS and a short stress test through a potentiostat.
  [AMPERE-2](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5DD00180C) (Digital Discovery 2025) is open hardware for
  exactly this: an OT-2, an Arduino and a Squidstat Plus, with a 15-well plating cell, a flush tool
  on peristaltic pumps, chloride baths of seven metals, and CV, EIS and chronopotentiometry in KOH
  ([code and BOM](https://github.com/AccelerationConsortium/SDL1_OpenTron_electrodeposition), as
  summarised in byu-vcl PR 270). The lab's org profile already names it as its frugal-twin example.
- **Change to the robot.** AMPERE-2's minimum bill of materials is about 5,000 (currency not stated;
  mostly amazon.ca links), not counting the robot and potentiostat. The lab has potentiostat
  threads for a Squidstat Plus (byu-vcl issue 26) and an EZstat Pro (PR 291). Plating baths are
  aqueous, so no hood is needed. Nickel and cobalt salts are carcinogens and need EHS sign-off.
- **What's new.** Initial activity is well covered: AMPERE-2 and FastCat (500+ Ni-based OER
  catalysts, [10.1002/aidi.202500138](https://doi.org/10.1002/aidi.202500138)) for OER, and CatBot
  ([10.1039/D5DD00403A](https://doi.org/10.1039/D5DD00403A)) for HER. Stability has been measured
  too: the first AMPERE ([10.1039/d4dd00203b](https://doi.org/10.1039/d4dd00203b)) used a liquid
  handler and modular reactors to measure metal dissolution. What this search didn't find is one
  closed loop that trades activity against loss after an accelerated stress test, so check that
  before claiming it. Two other additions: plating-bath checks by UV-Vis and conductivity with
  Jason Porter, which byu-vcl PR 270 found no automated setup doing, and remote access.
- **Where it can go.** AMPERE-2's baths draw on seven metal chlorides, so five-element
  (high-entropy) films are within reach, though co-depositing all five has to be shown. That puts
  the work inside Topic 15 of the FY2027 DoD MURI, *High-Entropy Electrochemical Material*, which
  asks how ensemble properties emerge from multi-element interactions.
- **Fit.** The strongest of the six: autonomous electrochemistry, frugal twins and Bayesian
  optimisation all in one, and electrodeposited films are alloys. It also gives the OT-2 the
  electrochemistry the CubXL list had pencilled in as its use-case 2, leaving the CubXL free for the
  corrosion work in the ONR YIP.
- **Risk.** Film-to-film reproducibility (substrate prep, bubbles, bath ageing). AMPERE-2's own
  repeatability numbers are the bar to match first.
- **Funding.** [ARO Early Career Program](https://www.grants.gov/search-results-detail/344592)
  (rolling, ≤ $120K/yr for 3 yr, Electrochemistry); NSF CBET 26-518, Chemical Process Systems (any
  time); the FY2027 MURI Topic 15 team (white paper Nov 20, proposal Apr 9, 2027); DOE BES Catalysis
  Science (Dec 14, 2026); ECS Toyota Young Investigator Fellowship (~Apr 30, 2027, unverified);
  BYU IDR with Porter.

### 2. Lithium, magnesium and boron from Great Salt Lake and produced-water brines

- **Question.** How selectively can sequential precipitation (Mg(OH)₂, then Li₂CO₃ or Li₃PO₄) or a
  sorbent pull Li, Mg and B from real brines, and how well do the Pitzer models predict each step?
  These are major-ion systems with well-tested parameters, so the work tests models with
  equilibrium measurements and identified solids, which is what Reviewer B asked for, rather than
  fitting trace-ion terms.
- **What the OT-2 does.** Builds dilution and reagent-dose series of brine in sealed vials or capped
  deep-well plates, ages them on the heater-shaker, samples supernatant for ICP-OES, and runs
  batch-sorption isotherms on sorbent powders. ICP-OES dilutions should be gravimetric and
  matrix-matched with an internal standard, the fixes the review digest lists for Reviewer B's
  ICP-OES point.
- **Change to the robot.** Almost none: sealed labware, a filter plate or centrifuge step, ICP-OES
  time. Solid identification (XRD) for anything that precipitates.
- **Fit.** Reuses the brine chemistry, PHREEQC work and student training from the PRF project, so it
  starts fastest. It is Utah-specific, and critical minerals are a federal priority. The materials
  angle is the sorbent (ion sieves, layered double hydroxides), which can come from direction 3.
- **Closest precedent.** [Ritchhart et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6mh00475j) (Materials
  Horizons) recovered critical materials from produced water and magnet leachates by selective
  precipitation, with AI agents driving automated instruments. Cite it. It's also a possible
  collaborator. What sets this work apart is hypersaline lake brine, identified solids, and
  equilibrium data checked against Pitzer/PHREEQC predictions.
- **Risk.** Real-brine matrices are hard on ICP-OES (Na and Mg at high concentration). Lithium from
  brine is crowded at pilot scale; the open niche is fast, model-checked screening.
- **Funding.** Utah Great Salt Lake Research Grant (Nov 13, 2026); DOE BES Separation Science
  (white-paper target Oct 30, applications by Dec 11, 2026; mechanism, not optimisation); NSF CBET
  26-518 EWRE or Chemical Process Systems, or NSF Chemistry CSS 26-519 (any time; send to one);
  Scialog SMMM (Mar 15, 2027); RCSA Team Science Accelerator LOI (Nov 9, 2026, team); MURI Topic 10
  (superconcentrated brines, team); BYU IDR; W. M. Keck (Jun 7, 2027) with a basic-science framing.

### 3. Co-precipitated oxide and hydroxide libraries

- **Question.** Map composition → phase → property for multicomponent (high-entropy) oxides made
  by co-precipitation or sol-gel routes, for example spinel or rock-salt OER catalysts (feeds
  direction 1) or dopants in Li-ion cathode precursors.
- **What the OT-2 does.** Dispenses mixed-metal precursor solutions and precipitant into vials or
  crucible arrays, ages them on the heater-shaker, and hands off to drying, calcination and XRD.
  [Velasco et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.202102301) and
  [Kumbhakar et al. 2023](https://doi.org/10.1002/aenm.202204337) built high-entropy oxide libraries
  this way, and the McCalla group's [2026 Adv. Mater. paper](https://doi.org/10.1002/adma.202519790)
  had an Opentrons dispense metal and citric-acid solutions into 64 alumina cups per round of a
  closed-loop, multi-objective search over cathode dopants.
- **Change to the robot.** Crucible or vial racks, a furnace (the lab builds furnaces) and campus XRD.
  The bottleneck is characterisation, not liquid handling.
- **Fit.** The most "materials science" of the six, and the direct heir of the precipitation
  know-how. Phase–property maps from noisy, expensive measurements are a natural Bayesian
  optimisation problem.
- **Risk.** XRD throughput and drying/calcination steps that the OT-2 doesn't automate. Start with a
  ternary to prove the loop before going high-entropy.
- **Funding.** NSF MPS Materials Research Programs 26-521 (any time; one proposal per PI per
  fiscal year); MURI Topic 15 with direction 1; DOE BES Materials Chemistry (Feb 1, 2027), pitched as
  a mechanism question.

### 4. Nucleation statistics and crystallisation inhibitors (the current setup, re-aimed)

- **Question.** What controls the *distribution* of induction times for BaSO₄, CaCO₃ and gypsum
  nucleation as supersaturation, ionic strength and additives change? Nucleation is stochastic, so a
  single onset per condition means little; dozens of replicate wells per condition do, and that is
  what a liquid handler is for.
- **Why this one.** Reviewer B's sharpest point was that a turbidity onset measures nucleation, not
  solubility. The current protocols, plates and camera already measure nucleation. This makes that
  the question instead of a flaw.
- **Change to the robot.** Filtered stocks, sealed plates and kinetic reads (campus plate reader, or
  the camera at fixed lighting). Fill-time offsets have to be logged per well (issue 80's checklist).
- **Fit.** Weaker on materials than 1–3, but it is the fastest route to a publishable dataset and
  keeps a petroleum framing (scale inhibition) alive for PRF or industry, alongside desalination and
  biomineralisation framings.
- **Precedent.** The precedent search found no Opentrons work on inorganic-salt nucleation or scale
  inhibitors. The nearest is [Alexander et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1002/aisy.70549): an OT-2 and a
  plate reader measuring polymer cloud points by turbidity. That is a case where turbidity is the
  right observable, because a cloud point is a phase boundary.
- **Risk.** Heterogeneous nucleation on dust and plate walls can swamp the chemistry. It needs blanks
  and replicate plates.
- **Funding.** NSF CBET 26-518 (Transport Phenomena covers physicochemical phenomena at fluid
  interfaces) or NSF Chemistry CSS 26-519 (any time); W. M. Keck (Jun 7, 2027); ACS PRF DNI with a
  scale-inhibition framing, but only as a genuinely new proposal with data behind it (PR 82 advises
  saving the two remaining tries).

### 5. The solution engine for the lab's corrosion work (no new proposal)

- The CubXL will run droplet-cell corrosion on the lab's copper (nickel–aluminium bronze for the ONR
  YIP) and AlSi10Mg. Each test needs an electrolyte, and each used droplet goes to ICP-OES.
- The OT-2 can make the electrolyte matrix (chloride, sulfate, pH, inhibitors such as
  benzotriazole for copper), make the matrix-matched ICP-OES dilutions, and run 96-well immersion
  screens of inhibitors on coupons or powders read by a colorimetric metal-ion assay.
- The precedent search found no Opentrons corrosion paper at all, so the plate screen is new
  ground. A 3D-printed multi-coupon cell would let the OT-2 run linear polarisation or EIS itself.
- **Funding.** No new proposal is needed. List the OT-2 in the ONR YIP and AFOSR YIP equipment pages
  as the solution and sample-prep station. If the plate screen works, the
  [ONR Long Range BAA](https://www.grants.gov/search-results-detail/356605) (open until Dec 31, 2026,
  or its successor) takes white papers on naval materials and corrosion.

### 6. Remote materials experiments for teaching

- The remote colour-matching OT-2 demo had run more than 1,000 experiments for dozens of users by
  August 2025 (org profile). Swap the paint for a real materials experiment that students can run from a
  browser: silver-nanoparticle colour, electrodeposition from direction 1, or the nucleation
  curves from direction 4.
- **Fit.** The cloud-lab theme exactly, and the education plan for NSF CAREER (Jul 28, 2027).
- **Funding.** NSF IUSE: EDU Level 1 (Jan 20, 2027, ≤ $400K); NSF CyberTraining Pilot (Jan 21,
  2027, ≤ $300K; needs a cyberinfrastructure co-PI); NSF PESOSE Track 1 (Mar 2, 2027); AWS and
  Anthropic research credits (rolling); a Sloan letter of inquiry (rolling, unverified); and, later,
  the education plan of an NSF CAREER proposal.

## How this was put together

- **Context.** The lab's [org profile](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/.github/blob/main/profile/README.md)
  for its thrusts and themes; issue 80 and the shopping list for the OT-2's hardware; byu-vcl issues
  133, 270, 278, 298 and 307 for the CubXL and its corrosion plans; and the digital-alloy-lab threads
  for the ONR YIP, the AFOSR YIP (full proposal Dec 4 if invited), NSF CMMI AMDO and DOE Early
  Career, so nothing here competes with the alloy submissions for the same program.
- **PR 82.** Its [funding notes](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/digital-wetlab/blob/3d6ba0a/grants/alternative-funding/README.md)
  and [review digest](https://github.com/vertical-cloud-lab/digital-wetlab/blob/3d6ba0a/grants/acs-prf/REVIEW_2026.md)
  were read first, and items they verified on 2026-10-24 or 2026-10-29 are reused rather than
  searched again.
- **Funding search.** Two searches ran in parallel on 2026-10-29, one for federal programs and one for
  foundations, societies, industry, Utah, BYU and cloud credits. Each date was read on the funder's
  own page; anything that couldn't be is marked unverified.
- **Literature.** A third search built the precedent table, checking each DOI on Crossref and that
  the paper really used an Opentrons robot. [`ot2_funders.md`](ot2_funders.md) comes from
  [`scripts/funding/ot2_funders.py`](../../scripts/funding/ot2_funders.py), which searches Europe PMC full
  text and reads funders from Crossref. OpenAlex would have covered more journals, but its free daily
  budget is shared per IP address and was already used up on the GitHub runner.
- **Not used.** Edison: the PR 82 job is using the Edison rate limit for its 48 claim queries. The
  lab's Raspberry Pis and robots: not reachable from Actions, and not needed.
