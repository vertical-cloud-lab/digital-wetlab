# Panel Selection Analysis — ACS PRF DNI Proposal

> **Question:** Which ACS PRF panel is the best fit for a proposal on determining Pitzer ion-interaction parameters for Fe²⁺–HS⁻ and Pb²⁺–HS⁻ systems via high-throughput precipitation mapping?

> **Panel descriptions below are from the official ACS PRF list, updated Nov 15, 2024.**

---

## Candidate Panels

### Panel 3 — Inorganic Chemistry ← **current selection**

> Coordination and organometallic chemistry of the main group elements and transition, lanthanide, and actinide metals; homogeneous catalysis (see the Surface Science panel for heterogeneous catalysis); activation and conversion of petrochemically-relevant feedstocks; small soluble clusters; new reactive metal-ligand platforms.

| Criterion | Fit | Notes |
|-----------|-----|-------|
| Subject matter | ✅ Partial | Fe²⁺ and Pb²⁺ are transition/main-group metals; metal–sulfide precipitation is inorganic chemistry |
| "Petrochemically-relevant feedstocks" | ⚠️ Stretch | The proposal studies petroleum-produced-water brines, not feedstock activation/conversion |
| "Small soluble clusters" | ✅ Partial | FeS/PbS nanoparticulate clusters form during nucleation |
| Core expertise of reviewers | ⚠️ Concern | Typical Panel 3 reviewers focus on synthesis, catalysis, and metal-ligand reactivity — not aqueous electrolyte thermodynamics |

**Summary:** The systems studied (Fe²⁺, Pb²⁺, HS⁻) are inorganic, but the core intellectual contribution — Pitzer parameter determination — is physical chemistry/thermodynamics. A Panel 3 reviewer may question whether the proposal belongs here (see [MOCK_REVIEWER_FEEDBACK.md](MOCK_REVIEWER_FEEDBACK.md), Reviewer 2).

---

### Panel 6 — Chemical Physics/Physical Chemistry

> Theoretical chemistry, including simulation, modeling, quantum, and statistical mechanics; optical, laser, and ultrafast spectroscopies, mass spectrometry; processes in gas and condensed phases, plasma, solvation, combustion.

| Criterion | Fit | Notes |
|-----------|-----|-------|
| "Simulation, modeling" | ✅ Strong | Pitzer model predictions, PHREEQC simulations, parameter regression |
| "Statistical mechanics" | ✅ Strong | The Pitzer formalism is derived from statistical-mechanical theory of electrolytes |
| "Solvation" | ✅ Direct | Ion-interaction parameters describe solvation/hydration in concentrated electrolytes |
| "Processes in condensed phases" | ✅ Direct | Precipitation from aqueous solution |
| Core expertise of reviewers | ✅ Good | Panel 6 reviewers would understand thermodynamic modeling, activity coefficients, and electrolyte theory |

**Summary:** Strong fit. The core science — parameterizing an electrolyte thermodynamic model — aligns directly with "simulation, modeling, statistical mechanics, solvation, and processes in condensed phases." Panel 6 reviewers are most likely to appreciate the fundamental thermodynamic contribution.

---

### Panel 9 — Chemical and Petroleum Engineering

> Engineering studies including fluid and multiphase flow dynamics and interfacial transport phenomena with related experiments and computations, including process operations.

| Criterion | Fit | Notes |
|-----------|-----|-------|
| Petroleum relevance | ✅ Strong | FeS/PbS scaling in produced waters is a major petroleum engineering problem |
| "Interfacial transport phenomena" | ✅ Partial | Precipitation involves nucleation at interfaces |
| "Related experiments and computations" | ✅ Partial | The project combines HTE experiments with Pitzer model computations |
| Fundamental vs. applied | ⚠️ Concern | The proposal emphasizes fundamental thermodynamic parameters, not engineering solutions or process operations |
| Core expertise of reviewers | ⚠️ Mixed | Panel 9 reviewers focus on engineering; they may undervalue the fundamental thermodynamics contribution |

**Summary:** The petroleum application is strong, but the proposal frames itself as fundamental science (Pitzer parameter determination), not an engineering study. ACS PRF favors fundamental research, and positioning the work as engineering could weaken its appeal.

### Other Panels Considered

| Panel | Reason excluded |
|-------|----------------|
| 2 — Geochemistry and Biogeochemical Cycling | Focused on isotope/organic/sedimentary geochemistry; does not cover aqueous electrolyte thermodynamics directly |
| 8 — Geology and Geophysics | Stratigraphy, sedimentology, geophysics — no overlap with solution chemistry |

---

## Recommendation

**Panel 6 (Chemical Physics/Physical Chemistry)** is the strongest scientific fit based on the updated Nov 2024 descriptions. The core contribution — determining Pitzer ion-interaction parameters via systematic experimental and computational analysis — maps directly to Panel 6's scope: "simulation, modeling, statistical mechanics, solvation, and processes in condensed phases."

**Panel 3 (Inorganic Chemistry)** remains a reasonable choice if the proposal emphasizes the inorganic systems (Fe²⁺, Pb²⁺, HS⁻, metal–sulfide precipitation) and frames the Pitzer parameterization as a tool for understanding inorganic solution chemistry.
