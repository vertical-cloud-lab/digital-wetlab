# Potential ACS PRF Panel Reviewers

> **Context:** This document identifies researchers who may serve on the ACS PRF Panel 3 (Inorganic Chemistry) review panel or be selected as ad-hoc reviewers for a DNI proposal on LLM-based agents for qualitative inorganic chemistry automation. The proposal involves using AI agents on Opentrons liquid-handling robots to perform and reason about qualitative inorganic analysis (precipitation, solubility, complexation, redox reactions for systematic cation/anion identification).

## How ACS PRF Selects Reviewers

- **Applicants suggest six peer reviewers** (names and emails) as part of the submission.
- The program officer (Dr. Allegra Liberman-Martin for Panel 3) selects additional reviewers from the broader community.
- **Prior ACS PRF grantees** are a natural pool — they are familiar with the program's standards and expectations.
- Each reviewer can only review **one proposal per cycle**.
- Reviewers must have **no conflicts of interest** with the PI (no former mentors, collaborators, students, or institutional colleagues).
- Panel 3 covers: coordination and organometallic chemistry, homogeneous catalysis, petrochemical feedstock activation, small soluble clusters, and new reactive metal-ligand platforms.
- Panel 6 (Chemical Physics/Physical Chemistry) also possible: covers theoretical chemistry, simulation/modeling, quantum and statistical mechanics.

## Identification Criteria

Potential reviewers were identified based on:

1. **Prior ACS PRF funding** (DNI/ND grantees from 2019–2024 across Panels 3, 5, and 6)
2. **Research overlap** with the proposal's core topics: inorganic chemistry, laboratory automation, AI/ML in chemistry, qualitative analysis, coordination chemistry, petroleum-relevant catalysis
3. **Common publication venues** (see [Relevant Journals](#relevant-journals) below)
4. **Active in ACS Division of Inorganic Chemistry** or related divisions
5. **Early-to-mid career faculty** who are most likely to serve on DNI review panels
6. **Self-driving lab / autonomous experimentation** researchers
7. **Computer vision in chemistry** researchers
8. **High-throughput experimentation** in inorganic/materials chemistry
9. **Bayesian optimization / active learning** for chemistry
10. **Chemistry education / digital lab** researchers

## Strategic Implications for the Proposal

### Who is the likely audience?
The primary reviewers will most likely be **inorganic chemists** — faculty at U.S. doctoral institutions who:
- Have received prior ACS PRF funding (especially Panel 3 DNI or ND grants)
- Work on coordination/organometallic chemistry, homogeneous catalysis, or small molecule activation
- Publish in *Inorganic Chemistry*, *JACS*, *Organometallics*, and *Dalton Transactions*
- May have limited familiarity with AI/ML or laboratory automation

### What this means for proposal framing:
1. **Lead with the chemistry, not the AI** — Reviewers will evaluate scientific merit first; the inorganic chemistry must be compelling on its own
2. **Frame AI/automation as a tool, not the goal** — Position LLM agents as enabling fundamental chemical investigations (concentration-dependent equilibria, systematic ion identification), not as the research contribution itself
3. **Use familiar inorganic chemistry language** — Describe reactions in terms of solubility products, coordination equilibria, precipitation chemistry, and complexation constants
4. **Connect to petroleum relevance concretely** — Mention specific petroleum-associated metals (Ba²⁺ in produced water, Pb²⁺ in refinery streams) and petroleum processing contexts
5. **Cite broadly from the relevant journals** — Reference *Inorganic Chemistry*, *JACS*, and *Dalton Transactions* to signal alignment with the reviewing community
6. **Explain AI/LLM concepts accessibly** — Don't assume reviewers know what an LLM agent is; provide brief, clear explanations
7. **Highlight student training** — PRF prioritizes student education; describe concrete plans for graduate and undergraduate involvement in both the chemistry and the computation

### Suggested reviewers to nominate (six required):
When selecting the six suggested peer reviewers for the submission, aim for:
- **5 inorganic/petroleum chemists** who work on related chemistry AND have ACS PRF ties (ion identification, aqueous equilibria, coordination chemistry, catalysis)
- **1 researcher** at the AI/chemistry intersection who also has **ACS PRF funding** (e.g., Junwei Lucas Bao, Milad Abolhasani)
- **No conflicts of interest** — no former mentors, collaborators, students, or colleagues
- **Mix of career stages** — include both established and early-career faculty
- **Diverse institutions** — spread across different universities and regions

> **Note:** Only nominate AI/automation researchers who have confirmed ACS PRF funding (such as Bao, Abolhasani, Head-Marsden, Beykal, or Fang Liu) — those without PRF ties (White, Kulik, Coley, etc.) are unlikely to be on the panel.
