# ACS PRF Doctoral New Investigator (DNI) Grant — Comprehensive Guide

> **Spring 2026 Submission Window: February 9 – March 6, 2026 (5:00 PM EST)**

---

## 1. Program Overview

The ACS Petroleum Research Fund (PRF) is an endowed fund managed by the American Chemical Society that supports fundamental research directly related to petroleum or fossil fuels at nonprofit institutions. PRF grants are intended as **seed money** to enable an investigator to initiate a **new research direction**.

- **Award:** $110,000 over 2 years
- **Estimated awards per year:** ~75
- **Budget year:** September 1 – August 31
- **Earliest start date for Spring proposals:** July 1
- In 2024, ACS PRF awarded 195 research grants totaling ~$20.9 million

---

## 2. DNI Eligibility Criteria

### Who Can Apply

- **U.S.-based** scientists and engineers within the **first three years** of their first academic appointment at the rank of **Assistant Professor** (or equivalent)
- Must be at a **doctoral-granting institution** (PI must be eligible to supervise graduate students)
- Must be authorized by the institution to serve as PI on externally funded grants
- Appointment must promise reasonable continuity of service through the grant period
- Tenure-track faculty automatically meet these criteria

### Exceptions

- Tenure-clock extensions due to career-interrupting circumstances (parental leave, medical leave, etc.) within the first three years may extend eligibility
- Requires a confirmation letter from institutional leadership ([template PDF](https://www.acs.org/content/dam/acsorg/funding/grants/prf/prf-exception-template-jan2026.pdf))
- Extension matches the duration granted by the institution

### Limitations

- May submit **only one** proposal per meeting
- Maximum of **three (3) DNI applications** considered in a career, or **one (1) DNI funded**
- Cannot hold more than one active PRF grant at a time (including grants on time extension)
- After DNI eligibility is exhausted, may apply to the **New Directions (ND)** program

### Not Required

- ACS membership is **not required**
- Applicant does not need to be a chemist — any qualifying faculty member can apply

## 3. Research Scope Requirements

### Must Be Fundamental Research

The research must be **fundamental** — addressing basic causes and consequences of phenomena, with results of broad interest and applicability. The following are **excluded**:

- Applied research or methods development
- Research aiming to develop new experimental techniques or devices
- Medical, environmental, or social research
- Development of patentable content or commercialization
- Alternative energy (batteries, fuel cells, biomass, hydrogen, solar)
- Biosystems, biochemistry, biomedical/pharmaceutical
- Environmental remediation, pollution
- Non-petroleum topics

### Must Be Petroleum-Relevant

The research must relate to the "petroleum field" as defined by the PRF Trust: *"petroleum, natural gas, coal, shale, tar sands and like materials"* — their exploration, production, transportation, refining, and substitutes thereof.

- **Machine learning methods are welcome** in any scientific area
- PRF currently funds many topics in **sustainability and green chemistry** (e.g., new catalysts, methane/CO₂ upgrading/utilization)
- Contact a program officer to confirm relevance before preparing a proposal

### Must Be a New Direction

- Research must be **different from** the PI's graduate/postdoctoral work
- Must not be a "mere extension" of prior research
- Should not overlap with other funded projects
- PI should not have published or received other funding for the proposed research
- Limited or no preliminary results are expected — this is seed funding

## 4. Proposal Format Requirements

### Document Format

- **Word limit:** 4,000 words (excludes the 250-word abstract, figure/table captions, and references)
- **Spacing:** Double-spaced
- **Font:** 12-point (Arial, Times Roman, or Courier)
- **Submission format:** PDF uploaded through the [ACS PRF submission portal](https://prfgrant.acs.org/s/)

### Required Elements

1. **Abstract** (250 words, entered separately in the online system)
2. **Petroleum relevance statement** (100 words, entered separately online)
3. **Proposal body** (≤ 4,000 words) describing a single research project with a clear, testable hypothesis
4. **Budget and budget justification**
5. **PI biosketch/CV**
6. **Six suggested peer reviewers** (names and email addresses)
7. **Resubmission summary** (if applicable, entered online — not counted in word limit)

### Compliance Check

All proposals undergo a compliance check for:

- Completeness and correctness
- Fundamental nature of the research topic
- Relevance to petroleum or fossil fuels
- Extent to which the proposed research differs from prior graduate/postdoctoral research

Proposals failing compliance are **denied without external review**.

## 5. Budget Guidelines

### Total: $110,000 over 2 years

The budget may be divided among years according to project needs.

| Category | Limit |
|----------|-------|
| **Student support** | **≥ 60% of total budget** (≥ $66,000) |
| PI summer stipend | ≤ $8,000/year including fringe benefits ($16,000 max total) |
| Conference travel | ≤ $2,000/year ($4,000 total) — PI and students |
| Capital equipment | ≤ $5,000 (discouraged; requires justification and institutional cost-matching info) |
| Publication costs | ≤ $2,000 total (open access fees, language editing, covers) |

### Student Support (≥ 60%)

"Students" includes graduate students, undergraduates, and postdoctoral fellows. Eligible categories:

- Stipends/salaries/benefits
- Graduate tuition
- Field work (transportation, lodging — must be described in budget justification)

Funds can be shifted between student levels (grad → undergrad → postdoc) without prior approval but **cannot** be shifted out of student support.

### Unallowable Costs

- **No overhead/indirect costs** allowed
- No secretarial/administrative salaries
- No laboratory technicians, contractors, consultants, or visiting faculty
- No fees for enhanced communications services and technology

## 6. Funding/Review Criteria (in order of importance)

1. **Scientific merit** — Overall quality, significance, and potential to increase basic knowledge and/or stimulate additional research
2. **Student education** — Extent to which advanced scientific education is enhanced through student involvement
3. **Newness** — Originality of the proposed research for the PI
4. **Impact** — Effect of PRF funding on the PI's overall research program
5. **Qualifications & facilities** — Potential of the PI and adequacy of facilities

## 7. Patent Policy

From the PRF Trust agreement: *"None of the results of any such research shall be turned into profit by the recipient"* and *"Every patent...shall be immediately dedicated to the public, royalty free."*

## 8. Common Errors to Avoid

### Scope Issues

- Research topic is outside the petroleum field (most common reason for rejection without review)
- Research is applied, not fundamental
- Petroleum relevance statement is too vague or doesn't connect to the PRF Trust definition
- Using "petroleum industry" framing — PRF funds fundamental science, not industry applications

### Proposal Quality

- Multiple unrelated project ideas instead of a single focused research project
- No clear, testable hypothesis
- Overly ambitious scope for $110K/2 years
- Poorly crafted or "noncompetitive" proposals may be denied without external review

### Administrative

- Using an outdated application form
- Exceeding word/page limits
- Incomplete submission (missing required documents)
- No late materials accepted after the deadline
- Incorrect budget (wrong total amount, insufficient student support, exceeding PI salary cap)
- Not a "new direction" — overlaps with prior work or other funded projects

## 9. Tips for a Competitive Proposal

### Framing for This Project

The Digital Wetlab project (LLM-based agents for qualitative inorganic chemistry on Opentrons robots) would need to be framed around petroleum-relevant aspects. Possible angles:

- **Fundamental inorganic analytical chemistry** — systematic study of metal ion identification reactions, solubility equilibria, and precipitate characterization relevant to petroleum-associated metals (e.g., barium, lead in produced water)
- **Petrochemically-relevant feedstock analysis** — ion detection and characterization methods applicable to monitoring petroleum processing streams
- **Concentration-dependent reaction equilibria** — generating fundamental data on precipitation reactions, complexation, and equilibria relevant to mineral scaling in petroleum extraction

### General Tips

1. **Contact the program officer first** — Dr. Allegra Liberman-Martin (Panel 3, Inorganic Chemistry) at [a_liberman@acs.org](mailto:a_liberman@acs.org) can confirm fit and advise on scope
2. **Emphasize fundamentals** — Frame as basic science generating fundamental knowledge, not as developing a new tool or device
3. **Highlight the "new direction"** — Clearly distinguish from any prior work
4. **Student training is critical** — PRF prioritizes student involvement; describe specific plans for undergraduate/graduate student research experiences
5. **Keep scope manageable** — $110K over 2 years is seed money; focus on proof-of-concept/preliminary results
6. **Aim for preliminary data for larger grants** — Position this as generating data for a subsequent NSF CAREER or CHE proposal
7. **Single focused hypothesis** — Avoid multiple project ideas
8. **Review past grantees** — Check the [2024 grantees list](https://www.acs.org/content/dam/acsorg/funding/grants/prf/annual-report/annual-report-2024/grants-awarded-2024.pdf) for successful examples in your area

### Proposal Structure Suggestions

1. **Introduction** — Context and significance (petroleum relevance)
2. **Objectives / Specific Aims** — Clear, testable hypothesis
3. **Background** — Current state of knowledge, gap being addressed
4. **Research Plan / Approach** — Detailed methodology with timeline
5. **Expected Results and Impact** — What success looks like and its broader impact
6. **References**

## 12. Also Consider: New Directions (ND) Grant

If the PI does not meet DNI eligibility (e.g., beyond 3 years of first appointment, or already used DNI attempts), the **New Directions (ND)** program is the alternative:

- **Award:** $125,000 over 2 years
- **~75 awards/year**
- Same fundamental research and petroleum relevance requirements
- Allows **one co-PI** (co-PI's institution receives subcontract)
- Same budget structure (≥ 60% student support, same PI salary caps)
- ND emphasizes **"newness"** as the #1 review criterion
- Available to investigators in [eligible countries worldwide](https://www.acs.org/content/dam/acsorg/funding/grants/prf/prf-eligible-countries-2026.pdf)
