Question: Are there isopiestic, vapor-pressure, osmotic-coefficient, freezing-point or EMF data for aqueous lead(II) acetate or iron(II) acetate solutions, and have Pitzer parameters been derived for Pb(OAc)2 or Fe(OAc)2?

## Assessment

**For neither aqueous Pb(OAc)₂ nor Fe(OAc)₂ did this search identify a direct isopiestic, water-vapor-pressure, osmotic-coefficient, or freezing-point data set, or a published binary-electrolyte Pitzer parameter set** (β⁽⁰⁾, β⁽¹⁾ and Cφ for Pb²⁺–OAc⁻ or Fe²⁺–OAc⁻). This is a *not-located* finding, not proof that unpublished or difficult-to-index historical measurements do not exist. The closest identifiable studies require careful distinction from the requested binary solutions. **Lead(II) acetate—EMF data do exist, but not of the needed kind.** Bates, Edelstein and Acree (1946) compared solid-Pb and saturated-Pb-amalgam electrode potentials in acidified lead-acetate electrolyte. Their preliminary comparisons used 0.05 M Pb(OAc)₂; their temperature-series cells included 0.5 M Pb(OAc)₂ with a little acetic acid. They also cite an earlier Gerke electrode comparison in 1 M lead acetate. These measurements characterize the *difference between lead electrode materials* in a shared electrolyte, rather than EMF versus Pb(OAc)₂ concentration from which a salt activity coefficient could be obtained. (bates1946reproducibilityofthe pages 1-3, bates1946reproducibilityofthe pages 3-6, bates1946reproducibilityofthe media 00559469, bates1946reproducibilityofthe pages 7-11)

**Iron(II) acetate—acetate-containing electrochemistry is not equivalent to binary Fe(OAc)₂ data.** Buckingham *et al.* (2021) studied an Fe–acetate thermogalvanic system, but prepared it from FeCl₂ and FeCl₃ with added acetic acid and sodium acetate. Its measured electrochemical properties therefore pertain to a mixed-oxidation-state, multicomponent solution, not Fe(OAc)₂–H₂O osmotic behavior. (buckingham2021developingironbasedanionic pages 2-3, buckingham2021developingironbasedanionic pages 4-5)

**The identifiable Pitzer near-matches are different chemicals.** Arrad (2019) fitted a Pitzer model for **Pb(NO₃)₂**, using published osmotic coefficients and saturated-vapor-pressure data; it does not establish parameters for Pb(OAc)₂. Jang and Nemer’s iron study concerns Pitzer interactions involving **Fe–citrate** in NaCl brines, not Fe²⁺–acetate. An acetate-complex formation constant, even if available, is a speciation equilibrium constant—not a substitute for binary-salt Pitzer interaction parameters. (arrad2019thermodynamicmodelingof pages 1-2, jang2015derivationofpitzera pages 1-1)
