Question: What thermodynamic data exist for aqueous lead(II) acetate and iron(II) acetate above 25 C, such as stability constants, heat capacities, enthalpies, solubilities or activity coefficients?

## Thermodynamic data above 25 °C

**The best-documented data are aqueous complex-formation constants, not bulk properties of aqueous lead(II) acetate or iron(II) acetate solutions.** For Ac⁻ = CH₃COO⁻, the constants below describe M²⁺ + Ac⁻ ⇌ MAc⁺ and M²⁺ + 2Ac⁻ ⇌ M(Ac)₂(aq). β₂ is *cumulative*, not the stepwise constant for adding a second acetate. Giordano’s Pb constants are smoothed, infinite-dilution values extracted from experiments; the Fe constants are reproduced in a later compilation attributed to Palmer and Drummond (1988). (giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 1-2, giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility media 283b48e2, giordano1994organicliganddistribution pages 16-17, giordano1994organicliganddistribution pages 14-16)

**Lead(II).** Giordano measured dissolution of *anglesite (PbSO₄)* in equal-molality sodium acetate/acetic acid buffers at 25, 40, 55, 70 and 85 °C, then inferred the PbAc⁺ and Pb(Ac)₂(aq) constants shown above. The buffers contained approximately 0.001–0.25 mol kg⁻¹ of *each* component; pH was measured at room temperature, generally near 4.6. Thus, the reported dissolved-lead concentrations are **PbSO₄ solubilities in acetate-bearing water**, not saturation solubilities of crystalline lead acetate. Lead hydrolysis was important at 70–85 °C. (giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 1-2, giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 3-4, giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 2-3)

An independent study by Hennet and colleagues used potentiometric and polarographic measurements over approximately 24–84 °C. Giordano’s literature compilation lists, for example, polarographic log K₁ ranges of 2.08–2.17, 2.24–2.29 and 2.32–2.34 at 44, 64 and 84 °C, respectively, at ionic strength 0.1; **these conditional-medium values should not be substituted directly for Giordano’s infinite-dilution constants**. A subsequent review gives best-fit Pb constants based on both studies. Its Pb entries at 100, 150 and 200 °C are explicitly *extrapolations* from 25–85 °C data, not additional high-temperature measurements. (giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 6-7, giordano1994organicliganddistribution pages 14-16, giordano1994organicliganddistribution pages 16-17, giordano1994organicliganddistribution media a0eca3b1)

**Iron(II).** Giordano and Kharaka’s compilation attributes the FeAc⁺ and Fe(Ac)₂(aq) constants in the table to Palmer and Drummond, *Journal of Physical Chemistry* **92**, 6795–6800 (1988). It labels the hydrothermal Fe-acetate dataset experimentally determined, but the accessible compilation does not establish which individual tabulated temperatures were measurement points versus fitted values; it supplies neither uncertainties nor the original pressure and supporting-electrolyte conditions. Palmer and Hyde’s later paper, “An experimental determination of ferrous chloride and acetate complexation in aqueous solutions to 300 °C,” *Geochimica et Cosmochimica Acta* **57**, 1393–1408 (1993), is another relevant primary source, but its results could not be verified from accessible full text here. The tabulated species are **dissolved Fe(II) complexes**, not solubilities of an Fe(II)-acetate solid. (giordano1994organicliganddistribution pages 14-16, giordano1994organicliganddistribution pages 16-17)

### Enthalpies, heat capacities, activities and salt solubilities

* **Formation enthalpy:** The temperature dependence of β permits a *model-dependent van’t Hoff estimate* of a complex-formation enthalpy, but the accessible tables do not supply a directly measured calorimetric ΔH or a defensible temperature-dependent ΔH/ΔCₚ for either metal–acetate complex. In particular, the **10.7 kcal mol⁻¹** used in Giordano’s calculations is an adopted **lead-hydrolysis** enthalpy, *not* a lead–acetate formation enthalpy. (giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 4-5, giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 3-4)
* **Heat capacities and activities:** No directly measured elevated-temperature heat capacities, partial-molal heat capacities, or osmotic/mean activity coefficients **for the binary Pb(CH₃COO)₂–H₂O or Fe(CH₃COO)₂–H₂O systems** were verified in the retrieved literature. Giordano *calculated* activity coefficients to reduce his PbSO₄-solubility data; because elevated-temperature NaAc ion-interaction parameters were unavailable, he reused **25 °C** parameters at 40–85 °C. Those are modeling assumptions, not measured activity coefficients of lead acetate. (giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 4-5, giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 1-2, giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 5-6)
* **Solubility of the acetate salts:** No verified temperature series for saturation against a specified crystalline lead(II)- or iron(II)-acetate hydrate was found in the accessible sources. Giordano’s measured Pb concentrations are useful for *acetate-enhanced anglesite dissolution* but cannot be presented as a lead-acetate-salt solubility curve. A salt-solubility calculation would additionally require the identity and thermodynamics of the stable solid phase and a concentration-dependent aqueous activity model. (giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 1-2, giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 3-4, giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 2-3)

**Bottom line:** Temperature-dependent **speciation constants exist for both metals**—directly documented through 85 °C for Pb and tabulated from hydrothermal Fe work through 300 °C. The searched, accessible evidence is substantially thinner for the requested **solution heat capacities, calorimetric enthalpies, measured activity coefficients and acetate-solid solubilities**; their absence here is a literature-verification gap, not proof that no such measurements have ever been made. (giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 1-2, giordano1994organicliganddistribution pages 14-16, giordano1994organicliganddistribution pages 16-17)
