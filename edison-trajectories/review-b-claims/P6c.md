Question: How did Paige et al. (1992) represent lead sulfate in their Pitzer model, and did they use a beta(2) term to represent PbSO4 ion pairing instead of an explicit association constant?

Paige et al. (1992) represented the PbSO₄–H₂O system with Pitzer parameters for the Pb²⁺–SO₄²⁻ electrolyte. A later tabulation attributes to them β⁽⁰⁾ = 0, β⁽¹⁾ = 4.368, **β⁽²⁾ = 129.0**, and Cφ = 0, with α₁ = 1.4 and α₂ = 12. The β⁽²⁾ term enters the concentration-dependent cation–anion interaction coefficient; it is **not** a formation constant for Pb²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ ⇌ PbSO₄(aq). It can therefore account phenomenologically for strong lead–sulfate interactions, but does not itself specify how much neutral PbSO₄(aq) is present. (charykova2010thermodynamicsofarsenates pages 9-10, charykova2010thermodynamicsofarsenates pages 10-11)

**Did they use it *instead of* an explicit association constant?** The retrieved sources establish the β⁽²⁾ parameter but do not establish that Paige et al. omitted a separate aqueous PbSO₄ association equilibrium. The original 1992 paper was unavailable for inspection, so that stronger claim cannot be verified from the evidence examined. (charykova2010thermodynamicsofarsenates pages 9-10)
