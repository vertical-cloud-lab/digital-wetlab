Question: Have FeS or PbS solubilities in NaCl or other chloride brines been modeled successfully using only solubility products, aqueous complex constants (for example FeHS+, Pb(HS)2 aq, PbCl_n complexes) and activity coefficients of the background electrolyte, without any Fe-HS or Pb-HS ion-interaction (Pitzer) parameters?

## Conclusion

**Yes for PbS, in a qualified sense; not yet established for FeS under the precise restriction asked.** A published galena study reproduced measured lead solubilities in a concentrated mixed-chloride brine using a solubility product, aqueous Pb–chloride and Pb–bisulfide complex constants, and approximate activity coefficients—**without Pb–HS Pitzer interaction parameters**. It did not, however, demonstrate high-precision prediction across different brine compositions. The FeS sources examined provide solubility equilibria and models, but do not establish comparably explicit, validated chloride-brine predictions using only background-electrolyte activity coefficients and no Fe–HS interaction terms. (bourcier1987oresolutionchemistry; pages 13-14, bourcier1987oresolutionchemistry; pages 14-16, sun2008equilibriumexpressionsrelated pages 3-4, rickard2026thechemistryof pages 12-13)

Bourcier and Barnes measured galena in a calcite-buffered **5 m NaCl + 0.5 m CaCl₂** brine containing H₂S and CO₂. At 100, 152, 203, 254, 304, and 355 °C, measured Pb was **0.1, 0.1, 0.2, 1.0, 1.9, and 9.5 ppm**, versus **0.3, 0.3, 0.5, 0.8, 4.2, and 5.2 ppm** calculated using published galena, PbClₙ, and Pb–bisulfide equilibrium constants. Every prediction was within one order of magnitude; the calculated-to-measured ratios ranged from about 0.55 to 3. This is a meaningful test of the equilibrium-speciation approach, though only at one nominal mixed-brine composition. (bourcier1987oresolutionchemistry; pages 13-14, bourcier1987oresolutionchemistry; pages 14-16)

**The activity-model distinction matters.** That PbS calculation was *not* a background-NaCl-only Pitzer calculation. The paper specifies Helgeson’s **b-dot** method for charged species, unit activity coefficients for neutral aqueous species, and gas-solubility-derived treatment for H₂S; it also approximates NaHS electrolyte behavior using NaCl data. Its reported PbS calculation consequently contains no explicit Pb–HS *Pitzer* parameter, but it does rely on simplifying assumptions about trace-species activities in concentrated mixed electrolytes. The authors themselves characterize their activity coefficients as approximate. A Pb–bisulfide **formation constant** is not a Pb–HS **Pitzer interaction coefficient**: the former accounts for a defined complex, while the latter describes residual nonideality between dissolved species. (bourcier1987oresolutionchemistry; pages 4-5, bourcier1987oresolutionchemistry; pages 8-9, bourcier1987oresolutionchemistry; pages 21-22, bourcier1987oresolutionchemistry; pages 22-23)

For **FeS (mackinawite)**, expressions based on FeS + 2H⁺ ⇌ Fe²⁺ + H₂S, or equivalently FeS + H⁺ ⇌ Fe²⁺ + HS⁻, are documented, but an expression for its solubility product is not by itself a validation of *total dissolved iron* across chloride-brine concentrations. A recent review describes a Pitzer-based optimization of mackinawite thermodynamics without, in the accessible account, establishing which Fe–HS parameters were omitted. It also reports a higher-pH, approximately pH-independent solubility regime associated with neutral **FeS⁰(aq)**, so a model limited to FeHS⁺ need not capture every condition. Thus the evidence supports **“PbS has been modeled without such a Pitzer parameter at roughly factor-of-few accuracy under tested conditions,”** not the broader claim that FeS and PbS chloride-brine solubilities are generally predictable with background-electrolyte coefficients alone. (sun2008equilibriumexpressionsrelated pages 3-4, rickard2026thechemistryof pages 12-13)
