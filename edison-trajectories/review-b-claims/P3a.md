---

Question: When Pitzer parameters are fitted to solubility data for a ternary system (for example a sparingly soluble salt in NaCl solutions), can the binary and mixing parameters be resolved separately, or must the background-salt and mixing parameters be fixed from independent data? Are there published discussions of parameter correlation in such fits?

## Answer

**A ternary solubility curve generally does not uniquely separate all binary and mixing Pitzer parameters.** For a sparingly soluble salt in NaCl(aq), each measured solubility supplies a solid–solution equilibrium constraint involving dissolved-ion activities and, for a hydrate, water activity. The fitted curve therefore constrains the *combined* activity-model response, not automatically each NaCl binary coefficient, solute-ion coefficient, mixing coefficient, and solubility product independently. This is a practical identifiability problem, not a theorem that simultaneous fitting is impossible. (li2006measurementandchemical pages 33-37, gaans1991extractingrobustand pages 1-4, gaans1991extractingrobustand pages 4-7)

**What should be fixed?** The usual defensible sequence is to determine the **background NaCl binary parameters from independent NaCl thermodynamic data**, constrain other available single-electrolyte parameters and the solid equilibrium, and then fit *selected* mixed-ion parameters to mixed-system data. The mixing parameters **need not all be fixed from independent data**: a published calcium-sulfate modeling account describes Harvie and coworkers taking single-electrolyte parameters from an established parameterization **while fitting third-virial mixing parameters to ternary common-ion solubility data**; the resulting gypsum-in-NaCl calculation agreed with experiment. Conversely, if mixing terms are independently known, or are explicitly assumed negligible within the required accuracy, mixed-solution data can help determine an otherwise inaccessible single-electrolyte parameter. These are modeling choices that require validation, not interchangeable ways of assigning unique physical meaning to every fitted coefficient. (li2006measurementandchemical pages 89-93, gaans1991extractingrobustand pages 1-4)

**Published discussion of correlation:** Van Gaans, *Journal of Solution Chemistry* **20** (1991), 703–730, DOI **10.1007/BF00650718**, is directly about “ridge regression as an aid to the problem of intercorrelation” in Pitzer parameter estimation. It explains that the regression predictors for the different coefficients are all functions of concentration, so ordinary least squares can yield imprecise individual coefficients even when the observed property is fitted well. Correlation depends on the concentration range and experimental design; increasing the number of points does not remove its intrinsic component. Van Gaans states explicitly that single-electrolyte and mixing coefficients can **in principle** be obtained together from complex mixed solutions, but that inferring more parameters simultaneously increases mathematical difficulty and uncertainty. The paper illustrates the issue with GaCl₃–HCl volume and heat-capacity data—not a calcium-sulfate/NaCl solubility fit—so it supports the *general identifiability warning*, not a numerical correlation coefficient specific to that solubility system. (gaans1991extractingrobustand pages 1-4, gaans1991extractingrobustand pages 4-7, gaans1991extractingrobustand pages 9-12, gaans1991extractingrobustand pages 15-18)

**Bottom line:** Fix or strongly constrain background-salt binaries from independent measurements **if the aim is interpretable, transferable parameters**; fit only the mixing combinations that the ternary data can support, preferably alongside other compositions or measured properties. Do not claim that *all* mixing terms must already be known, or that an excellent solubility fit proves that simultaneously fitted binary and mixing terms have been separately resolved. (li2006measurementandchemical pages 89-93, gaans1991extractingrobustand pages 1-4, gaans1991extractingrobustand pages 9-12)
