Question: How large is the error from assuming water activity equals 1 in solubility-product calculations for minerals in concentrated NaCl brines (for example 1-6 mol/kg), particularly for hydrated solids or dissolution reactions that involve water?

## Water-activity error in NaCl brines

**Assuming water activity is 1 can cause a small error for reactions with no net water, but an order-of-magnitude error for water-rich hydrates.** For a dissolution reaction that *produces* \(n\) waters, write its reaction quotient as \(Q=\mathrm{IAP}\,a_w^n\), where IAP is the product of dissolved-species activities. Thus, at saturation, \(\mathrm{IAP}_{\rm sat}=K/a_w^n\). Setting \(a_w=1\) while retaining the same thermodynamic \(K\) overestimates \(Q\)—and the calculated saturation ratio—by \(a_w^{-n}\). It underestimates the **ion-activity product required for saturation** by the reciprocal factor. The resulting saturation-index bias is \(\Delta\mathrm{SI}=-n\log_{10}a_w\). This follows from the mass-action definition of activities; water’s activity must not be omitted when water appears in the reaction. (mistry2013effectofnonideal pages 3-4, mistry2013effectofnonideal pages 5-7)

For example, \(\mathrm{CaSO_4\cdot2H_2O(s)\rightleftharpoons Ca^{2+}+SO_4^{2-}+2H_2O}\) has \(n=2\); \(\mathrm{Na_2SO_4\cdot10H_2O(s)\rightleftharpoons2Na^++SO_4^{2-}+10H_2O}\) has \(n=10\). These are **water-term-only** corrections, with ion activities held fixed. Mistry and Lienhard give \(\ln a_w=-2mM_w\phi\) for aqueous NaCl, where \(m\) is its molality and \(\phi\) its osmotic coefficient; their experimental-data-based fit and plotted water activities cover the illustrative 1–6 mol kg⁻¹ range. (mistry2013effectofnonideal pages 5-7, mistry2013effectofnonideal media 776102b1, mistry2013effectofnonideal media ddaf0a4c)

At about **6 mol kg⁻¹ NaCl**, taking \(a_w\approx0.75\), omission makes the gypsum reaction quotient roughly **1.8-fold too high** (about **+0.25** saturation-index units); for a ten-water dissolution reaction it is roughly **17-fold too high** (about **+1.2** units). Around **1 mol kg⁻¹**, the corresponding errors are approximately **7%** and **40%**. If the chosen dissolution reaction has *no net water*, this particular direct factor is absent; water can still influence speciation and competing hydrate equilibria. (mistry2013effectofnonideal pages 5-7, mistry2013effectofnonideal media ddaf0a4c)

**Scope matters.** These factors do not predict the full change in dissolved-mineral concentration: NaCl also changes ionic activity coefficients, complexation, and potentially which solid phase is stable. Concentrated-brine mineral-solubility models therefore treat solution interactions and solid equilibria together rather than correcting water activity alone. Use water activity and ion activities at the actual temperature and brine composition, and check that the stated hydrate is the relevant solid. (weare2006technologyforincreasing pages 20-23, hovey1991thermodynamicsofaqueous pages 8-12, weare2006technologyforincreasing pages 30-32)
