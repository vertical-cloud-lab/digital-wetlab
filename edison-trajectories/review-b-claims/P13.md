Question: What are the main sources of inaccuracy when measuring dissolved Fe and Pb by ICP-OES in high-salinity brines (dilution error, matrix and easily ionized element effects), and what practices are recommended, such as gravimetric dilution, matrix matching, internal standards, standard addition or ICP-MS?

## Dissolved Fe and Pb in high-salinity brines by ICP-OES

The principal risk is that a calibration made in dilute acid does not represent a dense, Na/K-rich brine. In a study of 10% NaCl, KCl and related matrices that included Fe and Pb, recoveries across the tested elements were **60–95% without matrix correction**, compared with **92–102% using a Be internal standard**. Those ranges describe the study’s elements collectively, not Fe or Pb individually. (krejcova2007determinationofmetal pages 4-5)

**Dilution error.** A brine’s density makes its mass different from the same nominal volume of water; inaccurate transfer or an incorrect dilution-factor or density conversion biases the calculated original Fe and Pb concentrations. Weigh both the brine aliquot and diluent, record the *mass-to-mass* ratio, and measure brine density if reporting concentrations per litre of original brine. A study of concentrated natural brines used gravimetric 50–100-fold dilution and explicitly identified density conversion as important to the original-sample result. Dilution also raises the reporting limit after back-calculation. (moors2023thewaterbodiesof pages 6-7, krejcova2007determinationofmetal pages 3-4)

**Salt-matrix and optical error.** Dissolved salts change sample-introduction and plasma behavior, commonly suppressing emission relative to aqueous standards, although enhancement is possible. Salt deposits can obstruct a nebulizer; a high-solids-tolerant nebulizer, suitable humidification and validated robust plasma conditions help. Separately, high concentrations of other elements can raise or overlap the optical background: the documented example of Al increasing the background around the Pb 220.35-nm line illustrates why Pb peak profiles, background positions and an alternative line should be checked rather than relying solely on an internal standard. (krejcova2007determinationofmetal pages 3-4, hou2000inductivelycoupledplasmaoptical pages 5-7, miller2010atomicabsorptionspectroscopy pages 14-15, miller2010atomicabsorptionspectroscopy media 98dc2454)

**Easily ionized elements.** Abundant Na and K add a plasma-composition effect to the physical salt effect; Fe and Pb responses can therefore depend on the Na/K load, operating conditions and selected emission line. The cited salt experiment demonstrates the *net* bias but does not isolate an electron-density mechanism or establish a universal direction or correction factor for the particular Fe and Pb lines. Match the major-salt composition of calibrants where feasible, or use standard addition on representative brines to test the actual response. Add an internal standard to every blank, calibrant and sample, and verify that its response tracks the analytes: Be worked well in the cited Na/K-salt study, but should not be assumed suitable for every brine. (krejcova2007determinationofmetal pages 3-4, krejcova2007determinationofmetal pages 4-5, krejcova2007determinationofmetal pages 6-6)

**Practical validation and method choice.** Use independently prepared dilutions, preparation blanks, duplicate samples and matrix spikes; compare results at two dilution factors and confirm difficult matrices by standard addition. Establish the *brine-specific* Fe and Pb quantification limits after dilution. If Pb—or Fe—is below the validated ICP-OES limit, ICP-MS offers substantially greater instrumental sensitivity, but it is **not a cure for salinity**: sodium can also strongly suppress ICP-MS signals, so sufficient dilution or validated salt removal/preconcentration and matrix controls remain necessary. Published approximate ICP-AES/ICP-MS detection-limit comparisons are instrument figures, **not** attainable detection limits for an undiluted brine. (krejcova2007determinationofmetal pages 3-4, krejcova2007determinationofmetal pages 4-5, kawaguchi1987matrixeffectsin pages 1-3, miller2010atomicabsorptionspectroscopy media 942b7756, fischer2018ultratraceanalysisof pages 1-2)
