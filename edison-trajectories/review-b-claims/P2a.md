Question: How has the constant ionic medium (swamping electrolyte) method been used to determine solubility products and complex formation constants of sparingly soluble metal sulfides, or of Fe(II) and Pb(II) with acetate? Which studies measured FeS or PbS solubility in NaCl or NaClO4 media with or without added acetate?

## Method and interpretation

A **constant ionic medium** uses a large, fixed concentration of supporting electrolyte—often NaClO₄—while acid, dissolved metal, or a ligand is varied. Keeping the medium nearly unchanged limits variation in activity coefficients, so measured concentrations can be used to determine **conditional constants for that medium**. Converting those constants to thermodynamic, infinite-dilution values requires additional activity-coefficient corrections. NaCl is not necessarily inert toward Pb(II): chloride complexes must be included explicitly. (gamsjager1983thermodynamicaspectsof pages 1-4, clever1980thesolubilityof pages 20-21)

For an approximately stoichiometric iron monosulfide, the useful dissolution reaction is FeS(s) + H⁺ ⇌ Fe²⁺ + HS⁻, with an activity-based constant *K* = *a*(Fe²⁺)*a*(HS⁻)/*a*(H⁺). At fixed H₂S pressure, measuring dissolved Fe(II) and pH across otherwise comparable solutions tests the expected slope of −2 for log[Fe²⁺] versus pH and gives a solubility constant from the intercept. This is preferable to reporting *a*(Fe²⁺)*a*(S²⁻) without specifying the uncertain second dissociation constant of H₂S. The identity and aging of the solid, oxygen exclusion, and possible nonstoichiometry matter as much as the electrolyte. (davison1991thesolubilityof pages 1-3, davison1991thesolubilityof pages 3-6, clever1980thesolubilityof pages 21-23)

To obtain an acetate formation constant by a **solubility method**, first establish the baseline dissolution/speciation model in the chosen medium, then measure the change in total dissolved metal as free acetate varies. At fixed medium composition, a simple model is [M]ₜₒₜ/[M²⁺] = 1 + β₁[Ac⁻] + β₂[Ac⁻]² + …, **after accounting for** chloride, hydrolysis, sulfide, and other complexes. An acetate-dependent increase in total metal alone does not establish β₁ or β₂, much less a sulfide *K*sp, without those controls. Heindl and Gamsjäger explicitly cautioned that their sulfide-solubility data were relatively insensitive to weak dissolved complexes and recommended investigating such species separately. (heindl1977solubilityconstantsand pages 4-5, giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 4-5, clever1980thesolubilityof pages 20-21)

## Which solids and media were actually studied?

| Study | Measured solid and medium | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| **Heindl and Gamsjäger (1977)** | Metal-deficient **monoclinic pyrrhotite**, approximately Fe₀.₈₈S, in **1.00 mol kg⁻¹ NaClO₄**, at **50.7 °C** and controlled H₂S pressure; **no acetate reported**. | A continuously circulating, gas-controlled cell measured pH potentiometrically and total Fe(II) photometrically. The measured log([Fe(II)]ₜₒₜ × *P*H₂S)–pH slope was **−1.93 ± 0.06**; the authors reported **log *K* = 3.80 ± 0.10** for their specified proton-promoted dissolution reaction and medium. This is **not** a measurement of an Fe–acetate constant or an acetate-containing FeS solubility. (heindl1977solubilityconstantsand pages 1-4, heindl1977solubilityconstantsand pages 4-5) |
| **Gamsjäger, Reiterer and Heindl (1982)**, assessed by **Davison (1991)** and **Gamsjäger (1983)** | Synthetic hexagonal and natural monoclinic **pyrrhotite**, and **troilite (α-FeS)**, measured at approximately **50 °C in 1 mol kg⁻¹ NaClO₄**; **no acetate documented** in these accounts. | Extends fixed-perchlorate sulfide measurements to distinct Fe–S phases. Davison converted the results to 25 °C values of approximately **p*K* = 5.19** (synthetic hexagonal pyrrhotite), **5.17** (natural monoclinic pyrrhotite), and **5.18** (troilite) on his Fe²⁺–HS⁻/H⁺ convention. These converted numbers must **not** be equated directly with the original log *K* for the H₂S-pressure reaction. (davison1991thesolubilityof pages 3-6, davison1991thesolubilityof pages 6-8, gamsjager1983thermodynamicaspectsof pages 4-7) |
| **Nriagu**, as documented by **Clever and Johnston (1980)** | **PbS (galena)** in **3.0 molal NaCl–HCl–H₂O**, at **310.15–473.15 K**, pH approximately **2–5**; **no acetate reported**. | Direct PbS solubility measurements in concentrated chloride medium. Dissolved Pb was predominantly chloride-complexed; a calculation combining the PbS solubility product, Pb–chloride formation constants, and H₂S equilibria reproduced the observations approximately. It is **not** an experiment in NaClO₄ or an acetate–PbS experiment. (clever1980thesolubilityof pages 20-21) |
| **Kivalo and Ringbom (1956)**, reviewed by **Clever and Johnston (1980)** | **PbS** studied **polarographically in hydrochloric-acid medium**, with correction for Pb–chloride complexation; the cited account does **not** establish a fixed NaCl or NaClO₄ supporting-electrolyte concentration or added acetate. | An experimental route to a PbS solubility product, subject to the choice of H₂S dissociation constants; it should not be relabelled as an acetate or perchlorate measurement. (clever1980thesolubilityof pages 21-23) |
| **Giordano (1989)** | **Anglesite, PbSO₄—not PbS**—in equimolal **acetic acid/sodium acetate**, each **0.001–0.25 molal**, at **25–85 °C**; no fixed NaCl or NaClO₄ background is specified. | Extracted stability constants for **PbAc⁺** and **PbAc₂** from sulfate-solid solubility. The paper explicitly selected PbSO₄ rather than PbS to avoid galena’s very low solubility and H₂S/redox complications. Its abstract reports infinite-dilution **log β₁ = 2.4 ± 0.1** and cumulative **log β₂ = 3.4 ± 0.4** at 25 °C; these are **not PbS-derived constants**. (giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 1-2) |

**Bottom line.** The identified **NaClO₄ sulfide-solubility studies concern iron sulfides without added acetate**; the clearly documented **NaCl PbS study is Nriagu’s, also without acetate**. The retrieved acetate-based lead complexation experiment dissolved **PbSO₄**, not PbS. Separate potentiometric studies of **ferrous acetate** were identified bibliographically—Palmer and Drummond (1988), and Palmer and Hyde (1993)—but their primary texts were not available here; their medium conditions or numerical constants cannot be verified, and neither should be described as an FeS-solubility experiment. I did not find a verifiable primary study measuring **FeS or PbS solubility in fixed NaCl or NaClO₄ with added acetate**, or a verified NaClO₄–PbS solubility experiment; this is a limit of the retrieved evidence, not proof that no such study exists. (heindl1977solubilityconstantsand pages 1-4, davison1991thesolubilityof pages 3-6, clever1980thesolubilityof pages 20-21, giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 1-2)
