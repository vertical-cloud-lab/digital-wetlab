Question: Have temperature-dependent Pitzer parameters been published for aqueous sodium acetate and sodium propanoate, and what heat-capacity, enthalpy-of-dilution and vapor-pressure data support them?

## Conclusion

**A temperature-dependent Pitzer parameterization has been published for aqueous sodium acetate, but I did not identify an equivalent one for sodium propanoate (sodium propionate).** Beyer and Steiger’s 2002 sodium-acetate model gives temperature functions for β⁽⁰⁾, β⁽¹⁾, β⁽²⁾ and Cᵠ, with fitted coefficients tabulated for **255–398 K** and approximately **0–25 mol kg⁻¹**. By contrast, published sodium-propanoate Pitzer parameters cited in a later re-evaluation are **25 °C values**: β⁽⁰⁾ = 0.1875, β⁽¹⁾ = 0.2789 and Cᵠ = 0.01277, for concentrations up to about 3 mol kg⁻¹. Those values do not establish a temperature dependence. (beyer2002vapourpressuremeasurements pages 1-4, beyer2002vapourpressuremeasurements pages 10-12, partanen2011reevaluationofthe pages 3-4)

For **sodium acetate**, Beyer and Steiger fitted thermodynamic properties *simultaneously*, rather than inferring temperature dependence from vapor pressure alone. Their database includes 192 aqueous apparent-molar-heat-capacity measurements at **278–393 K** and **0.02–0.49 mol kg⁻¹**, alongside other solution heat-capacity datasets and 22 heat-capacity measurements of solid sodium acetate at **218–348 K**. Heats of dilution contribute three datasets: **289–293 K** at **0.03–2.2 mol kg⁻¹** (12 points), **293 K** at **0.3–14.9 mol kg⁻¹** (6 points), and **298 K** at **0.02–2.5 mol kg⁻¹** (2 points); heats of solution were also fitted. In the model, dilution enthalpy constrains temperature derivatives of the interaction parameters, while heat capacity constrains their second temperature derivatives. (beyer2002vapourpressuremeasurements pages 7-10, beyer2002vapourpressuremeasurements pages 10-12)

Their **new static vapor-pressure measurements** were made principally at **278.15–308.15 K** over approximately **0.65–6.47 mol kg⁻¹**, with some tabulated measurements at **313.15 K**. Vapor pressures were converted to water activities and osmotic coefficients; **52 of 83** vapor-pressure-derived values, spanning **278–313 K** and **1.9–6.4 mol kg⁻¹**, were retained in the fit. Earlier vapor-pressure datasets supplied additional osmotic-coefficient constraints at **273–288 K** and **373 K**. Isopiestic, freezing-point and boiling-point observations further extend the fitted temperature and concentration coverage. Thus, **255–398 K is the combined model’s range, not the range of the authors’ new vapor-pressure experiments**. (beyer2002vapourpressuremeasurements pages 1-4, beyer2002vapourpressuremeasurements pages 4-7, beyer2002vapourpressuremeasurements pages 10-12)

For **sodium propanoate**, the identified activity-data basis is principally **25 °C isopiestic work**, subsequently assessed by Partanen and Covington; their 2011 treatment fits Hückel rather than temperature-dependent Pitzer equations. Inglese, Sedlbauer and Wood did measure apparent molar heat capacities of **both** sodium salts from approximately **300 to 525 K at 28 MPa**, using sodium-acetate solution near **0.1209 mol kg⁻¹** and sodium-propanoate solutions at **0.3948** and **0.8974 mol kg⁻¹**. These are useful high-temperature calorimetric data, but they are **not the near-ambient-pressure calorimetric database used in the Beyer–Steiger acetate fit**, nor do they by themselves supply propanoate dilution enthalpies, vapor-pressure constraints or a published temperature-dependent Pitzer parameter set. No corresponding multi-temperature propanoate fit was established by the sources reviewed. (partanen2011reevaluationofthe pages 1-2, partanen2011reevaluationofthe pages 3-4, inglese1996apparentmolarheat pages 1-4, beyer2002vapourpressuremeasurements pages 10-12)
