Question: How do FeS and PbS solubility studies control H2S partial pressure, and what errors arise when H2S degasses from open vessels or microplates during sulfide precipitation experiments?

## Controlling H₂S during FeS and PbS studies

**A defined gas atmosphere and a sealed sulfide inventory are different controls.** In iron-monosulfide experiments, Wilkin and Barnes first purged the vessel with inert gas, then continuously supplied **3% H₂S in N₂** to maintain a constant H₂S partial pressure. They monitored pH and dissolved sulfide; the gas leaving the vessel was trapped in NaOH. This was an FeS aging and transformation experiment, rather than a direct FeS solubility-product measurement, but it demonstrates how an imposed gas composition holds the H₂S boundary condition steady. At fixed temperature, Henry’s law links dissolved molecular H₂S to its gas partial pressure; pH then determines how much is present as HS⁻. FeS solubility and supersaturation calculations must account for both. (wilkin1996pyriteformationby pages 2-3, sun2006kineticsofirona pages 96-98, sun2008equilibriumexpressionsrelated pages 3-4)

PbS studies also protect the *sulfide inventory* without necessarily imposing a fixed gas partial pressure. Uhler and Helz made bisulfide stock by bubbling H₂S through NaOH to saturation, measured its total sulfide, stored it in sealed vials, and injected reactants into **stoppered, N₂-flushed cuvettes** buffered at pH 8 for PbS precipitation measurements. In a galena solubility study, Villegas Jiménez and colleagues equilibrated PbS with measured sulfide additions in closed, Teflon-lined vessels under anoxic conditions, then measured **final** sulfide, pH, and dissolved Pb. Neither arrangement means that an externally specified *p*H₂S remained constant: in a closed vessel, gas–liquid partitioning and reaction can change headspace *p*H₂S while conserving the combined gas-plus-liquid sulfide inventory, provided the seal holds. (uhler1984precipitationofpbs pages 1-3, jimenez2000solubilitéduplomb pages 26-30, sun2006kineticsofirona pages 96-98)

## Errors caused by escape from open vessels or wells

An uncovered vessel or microplate well exposes H₂S to an effectively H₂S-free atmosphere. Escaping H₂S draws more dissolved sulfide through the H₂S ⇌ HS⁻ equilibrium, so the **actual sulfide dose and saturation state fall with time**; adding a known amount of Na₂S or NaHS does not fix *p*H₂S. The effect is especially consequential when a substantial fraction is molecular H₂S, and its magnitude depends on pH, mixing, exposed area, and time. It is not equivalent to removing only an inert gas: sulfide is a precipitating reagent. (jarosz2016analysisandfunctional pages 44-49, jarosz2016analysisandfunctional pages 49-57, manninen2021precipitationofmetal pages 9-13, sun2006kineticsofirona pages 96-98)

* **Precipitation yield and kinetics:** At controlled pH and otherwise comparable conditions, declining HS⁻ lowers the ion-activity ratio for FeS or PbS. A well can therefore show delayed or reduced turbidity and solid yield, or precipitation can cease sooner, for reasons mistaken for slow nucleation, inhibition, or a different solubility. If pH also drifts, its effect must be measured rather than assuming a fixed direction for every outcome. PbS precipitation work explicitly calculated supersaturation from Pb²⁺, HS⁻, and H⁺ activities; FeS calculations use the analogous ratio. (uhler1984precipitationofpbs pages 3-4, sun2006kineticsofirona pages 98-103, manninen2021precipitationofmetal pages 9-13)
* **Apparent solubility and mass balance:** An open experiment no longer has the sulfide inventory assumed by a closed-system equilibrium calculation. If solid is present and sulfide activity falls, it can dissolve toward a new equilibrium, increasing measured free metal under the simplified reaction MS(s) + H⁺ ⇌ M²⁺ + HS⁻ at fixed pH. Conversely, soluble metal–sulfide complexes can change *total* dissolved metal in another direction; neither total metal nor initial sulfide should be substituted for the **measured final free-ion activities** to infer a thermodynamic solubility product. The galena study measured final sulfide and checked reactor retention: recovery averaged **89 ± 11% after seven days**, even in capped vessels. Its investigators rejected separate experiments with depleted sulfide and anomalously high dissolved Pb rather than treating those results as reliable galena equilibria; they considered oxidation and solid/colloid artifacts as well, so those Pb results cannot be attributed solely to degassing. (jimenez2000solubilitéduplomb pages 46-51, jimenez2000solubilitéduplomb pages 53-56, jimenez2000solubilitéduplomb pages 56-60, manninen2021precipitationofmetal pages 31-35)
* **Microplate-specific artifacts:** Small wells can lose H₂S to their shared surroundings and transfer it between nominally independent wells. A microplate H₂S assay deliberately captures gas released from wells on a silver-coated cover and spaces samples apart to limit neighboring-well diffusion. Another study sealed 96-well plates with adhesive foil specifically to prevent **H₂S volatilization and cross-well contamination**. Thus an open plate can produce both a low, time-dependent sulfide exposure in source wells and unintended exposure in controls or adjacent conditions. Neither study supplies a universal percentage-loss correction for sulfide-precipitation microplates. (jarosz2016analysisandfunctional pages 49-57, balne2020characterizationofhydrogen pages 2-4)

The size of the problem is experimentally credible but **geometry-specific**: a NaHS study reports an approximately **5-minute H₂S half-life in open chambers** from prior literature, while its own drinking-bottle measurements found **47% less sulfide inside bottles after 12 hours** and **72% less after 24 hours**; bottle-tip losses were greater. These figures are warnings, **not measured loss rates for FeS/PbS microplates**. For interpretable precipitation experiments, use a known, maintained H₂S-containing gas atmosphere *or* a verified gas-tight, suitably small-headspace vessel; buffer and record pH, minimize opening, exclude oxygen, measure dissolved sulfide before and after incubation, and account for headspace H₂S if calculating an equilibrium constant. (ghasemi2023dissolvingsodiumhydrosulfide pages 1-2, ghasemi2023dissolvingsodiumhydrosulfide pages 3-5, jimenez2000solubilitéduplomb pages 46-51, sun2006kineticsofirona pages 96-98)
