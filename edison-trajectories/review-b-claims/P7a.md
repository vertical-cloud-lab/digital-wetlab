Question: In Pitzer models that include explicit ion pairs or complexes, how strongly are the association constants (for example K for PbOAc+ or CaSO4 aq) correlated with the binary interaction parameters for the same ions, and how has this non-uniqueness been handled?

## Association constants versus Pitzer binary parameters

**They can be strongly confounded in a fit, but the available studies do not establish a numerical correlation coefficient for either PbOAc⁺ formation versus Pb²⁺–acetate binary parameters or CaSO₄(aq) formation versus Ca²⁺–SO₄²⁻ binary parameters.** The reason is that explicit pairing changes the calculated free-ion concentrations, while Pitzer β terms can absorb some of the resulting concentration-dependent behaviour in bulk activity, osmotic, or solubility data. The two descriptions need not be mathematically identical over all conditions: the degree of confounding depends on the observables and concentration range. In particular, the β⁽²⁾ term used for 2:2 electrolytes has been described as a way to represent pairing *implicitly*. (turner2023sourcesofpitzer pages 3-6, vielma2019thermodynamicpropertiesof pages 36-39)

The distinction between evidence of confounding and a measured K–β correlation matters in the two examples:

Investigators control the ambiguity by fixing parameters, using independent speciation measurements, limiting model terms, and testing thermodynamic plausibility.*

For **PbOAc⁺**, Giordano derived lead–acetate stability constants from anglesite solubility, but did **not** jointly estimate the Pb²⁺–acetate binary β parameters. Instead, the analysis supplied background-salt interaction parameters and estimated activity coefficients, restricted the data used, determined the dominant one-acetate complex first, and then determined the two-acetate complex. The paper therefore illustrates how the fitting problem was constrained, not a measured correlation between its fitted K and a same-ion β. (giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 4-5, giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 3-4)

For **CaSO₄**, Shen and colleagues fitted an *interaction-only* Pitzer model rather than simultaneously fitting an aqueous-pair formation constant. Their Table 2 shows several distinct parameterizations with similar objective values—for example 0.12, 0.10, and 0.07 for models D, E, and F—so a good bulk-data fit alone did not select a unique parameterization. They set Cφ and β⁽⁰⁾ to zero and favoured a model whose inferred thermodynamic quantities were credible, rather than selecting solely by the lowest objective value. Separately, capillary electrophoresis supplied species-sensitive evidence for CaSO₄(aq), yielding **log₁₀ K_app = 1.34 ± 0.03** at 25.7 °C and ionic strength 0.1 mol L⁻¹. This is an *apparent, finite-ionic-strength* constant, not directly a Pitzer-model standard-state K; the study used specific-ion-interaction corrections for extrapolation. (shen2019thermodynamicmodelingof pages 4-5, shen2019thermodynamicmodelingof pages 3-4, philippini2009formationofcaso4(aq) pages 4-6, shen2019thermodynamicmodelingof media 12abeb67)

**How non-uniqueness has been handled:** investigators have fixed independently assessed interaction parameters before deriving complexation constants; measured association with a species-sensitive method rather than relying only on bulk thermodynamics; reduced the number of adjustable β terms; and checked fits against independent properties and physically plausible thermodynamic quantities. A combined Pb–chloride binding/Pitzer model offers a particularly direct warning: parameters fitted separately at four temperatures varied incoherently, suggesting overfitting. Turner and Clegg retained its 25 °C interaction parameters, rejected implausibly large complex–ion parameters, and stated that temperature dependence would require a simultaneous refit across temperatures. These are constraints on *practical identifiability*, not proof that K and β are exactly interchangeable. (turner2023sourcesofpitzer pages 9-13, giordano1989anglesite(pbso4)solubility pages 4-5, shen2019thermodynamicmodelingof pages 1-2, shen2019thermodynamicmodelingof pages 4-5, philippini2009formationofcaso4(aq) pages 4-6)
