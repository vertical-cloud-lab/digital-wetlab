Question: Up to what ionic strength does the Debye-Hueckel limiting law reproduce measured activity or osmotic coefficients within experimental accuracy, for fully dissociated 1:1 salts and for salts that hydrolyze or form ion pairs, such as PbCl2 or 2:1 transition-metal chlorides?

**At 25 °C, the practical limit for a fully dissociated aqueous 1:1 salt is approximately \(I<10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}\)** for the *bare* Debye–Hückel limiting law to reproduce mean activity coefficients to the accuracy of measurements. For NaCl or KCl, ionic strength equals salt molality. This is an approximate empirical boundary, not a sharp theoretical cutoff; the sources reviewed do not establish an equally general, experimental-accuracy cutoff for **osmotic** coefficients. Fitted extended Hückel equations can agree with both kinds of measurement at substantially higher concentrations, but that agreement must not be attributed to the limiting law. (partanen2010predictionofactivity pages 1-2, rowland2014thermodynamicsofstrong pages 1-5, partanen2020traceablevaluesfor pages 1-2)

For a nominally fully dissociated **2:1 chloride**, \(I=3m\), where \(m\) is salt molality; the 1:1 cutoff therefore cannot be transferred directly as a salt-*molality* cutoff. For **PbCl₂, CdCl₂, and other complexing or hydrolyzing salts**, there is no defensible universal maximum *nominal* ionic strength at which the uncorrected limiting law reproduces measured stoichiometric activity or osmotic coefficients within experimental accuracy. The equilibrium composition, rather than the formula-unit concentration alone, determines the free-ion ionic strength and number of dissolved particles. Simonin’s analysis gives \(\log_{10}\beta_1=1.50\) for PbCl⁺ and about 1.94 for CdCl⁺; it estimates that PbCl⁺ accounts for roughly 37% of dissolved lead at PbCl₂ saturation. Rard and Miller likewise describe CdCl₂ as appreciably complexed even at low concentrations. (rowland2014thermodynamicsofstrong pages 1-5, simonin2018determinationofthermodynamic pages 21-25, simonin2018determinationofthermodynamic pages 29-34, rard1985mutualdiffusioncoefficients pages 1-4)

**Thus, use about \(10^{-3}\ \mathrm{mol\,kg^{-1}}\) as the cited 1:1 activity-coefficient rule of thumb—not as a demonstrated accuracy limit for PbCl₂ or for osmotic coefficients.** For a speciating salt, the relevant test is whether a *speciation-corrected* limiting-law prediction agrees with a specified measurement uncertainty at the stated pH and concentration. The available PbCl₂ osmotic-coefficient comparison is with an explicit-complexation model, not with the bare limiting law, and does not supply a separate numerical cutoff. (partanen2010predictionofactivity pages 1-2, simonin2018determinationofthermodynamic pages 21-25, simonin2018determinationofthermodynamic pages 29-34, simonin2018determinationofthermodynamic media aa22eec5)
