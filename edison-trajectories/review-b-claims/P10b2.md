Question: How accurate are Pitzer ion-interaction models for concentrated salt systems and natural brines above an ionic strength of 2 mol/kg, for example the Harvie-Moller-Weare model, and where do they break down?

## Accuracy above 2 mol kg⁻¹

**There is no general breakdown at ionic strength 2 mol kg⁻¹.** A Pitzer ion-interaction model with parameters appropriate to the ions and conditions can predict equilibria well into the concentrated-brine range. Accuracy is **property-, composition-, temperature-, and database-specific**, however: an error measured for gypsum solubility cannot be assigned to every mineral, ion activity, or natural brine. The original Harvie–Møller–Weare (HMW) model describes the Na–K–Mg–Ca–H–Cl–SO₄–OH–HCO₃–CO₃–CO₂–H₂O system at **25 °C**; results at other temperatures rely on subsequent extensions or implementations. (reiss2021gypsumprecipitationunder pages 3-5, møller1986predictionofbrine pages 1-4)

The most useful reported benchmarks and qualifications are summarized below.

| Model / benchmark | Salinity and temperature | Reported performance | Important caveat |

Above about 1 molal, reliable like-charge and ternary interaction parameters become necessary. |
| **Original HMW: evaporating seawater** | Demonstrated through seawater evaporation to **I ≈ 20 mol kg⁻¹**; **25 °C** | Reported to reproduce mineral solubilities accurately over the evaporation sequence (reiss2021gypsumprecipitationunder pages 3-5) | The I = 20 result is a **maximum demonstrated for the modeled seawater-evaporation chemistry**, not evidence that every salt mixture or mineral is accurate to that ionic strength. |

The calculation used the HMW-based database plus later temperature handling. |

For a particularly clear numerical test, the HMW-based PHREEQC 2 database calculated **gypsum solubility mostly within 3%** of measurements in solutions containing **0–6 molal NaCl**; the compilation spanned 0.5–55 °C and the authors judged that discrepancy comparable to analytical uncertainty. The same database gave an **“excellent fit,” without a reported percentage error**, for measured gypsum solubility in Dead Sea–seawater mixtures at **I = 3–10 molal**, Ca/SO₄ ratios of **5–115**, and 15, 25, and 35 °C. Other reported tests include Dead Sea brine near **I = 10**, mixtures to **I = 13**, and mineral solubilities along seawater evaporation to **I = 20 molal**. These establish substantial *tested* high-salinity capability, not universal 3% performance to I = 20. (reiss2021gypsumprecipitationunder pages 3-5, reiss2021gypsumprecipitationunder pages 5-7)

Møller and Weare reported that solubilities in **well-determined ternary systems were fitted within 8%**, retaining agreement with binary electromotive-force and osmotic data. That is a **fit to parameterization data**, not an independent error estimate for arbitrary natural brines. A separate early geothermal-model report claimed mineral-solubility predictions usually within **5–10%** over **I = 0–20 molal**; it should likewise be read as a report about that model and its tested systems, not as a guarantee for every Pitzer database. (møller1986predictionofbrine pages 1-4, mollerweare1982calculationofthe pages 1-5)

## Where predictions break down

1. **Outside the calibrated composition or mineral set.** Above roughly 1 molal, oppositely charged binary interactions alone are generally insufficient: like-charge and ternary mixing parameters must be established from appropriate data. HMW’s major-ion success therefore does not automatically cover a new brine rich in other components. A Pitzer-based **BaSO₄/SrSO₄** extension, for example, gave barite and celestite solubilities better than **±10% at low-to-moderate concentrations but failed in highly concentrated waters**. A separate low-temperature parameterization had problems in **sulfate-dominated** systems and required reparameterization. These are documented **parameterization-specific failures**, not a failure of the Pitzer equations at one universal ionic strength. (møller1986predictionofbrine pages 1-4, garcia2005measurementandmodelling pages 42-44)

2. **When the database, rather than the formalism, is extrapolated.** Many interaction parameters were determined only at 25 °C or in simple mixtures. During evaporation toward near dryness, small mismatches can accumulate as composition changes. The HMW-based PHREEQC 2 database included a later temperature extension with **untested values**; its authors warned that calculations outside **0–60 °C** should be validated. More recent is not necessarily more accurate: for gypsum precipitation potentials in Dead Sea–seawater mixtures at **25 °C and 1 atm**, PHREEQC **3.3** predictions were **less accurate** than the earlier PHREEQC **2.1** HMW-based database. The comparison is shown in the retrieved Figure 1; changes made for elevated-temperature/pressure treatment included modifying the Pitzer short-range parameter. (reiss2021gypsumprecipitationunder pages 3-5, mariner2004indriftprecipitatessaltsmodel pages 277-280, reiss2021gypsumprecipitationunder pages 5-7, reiss2021gypsumprecipitationunder media bff7f687)

3. **When speciation, phase equilibria, or physical conditions are inadequately represented.** Strong association can require an appropriate additional interaction term or explicit complex species; carbonate predictions also depend on the supplied pH–CO₂ equilibria and mineral data. Gypsum and anhydrite exchange stability with increasing temperature and salinity, so calculating the wrong solid phase is not an activity-coefficient test. At roughly **above 300 °C**, particularly near boiling or critical conditions, rapidly changing density and dielectric properties, compressibility, phase separation, and stronger ion association defeat straightforward extrapolation of the nearly incompressible-liquid Pitzer representation; a suitable equation of state and species treatment are needed. (møller1986predictionofbrine pages 1-4, pitzer1986theoreticalconsiderationsof pages 3-5, reiss2021gypsumprecipitationunder pages 5-7, weare2006technologyforincreasing pages 15-17)

**Practical interpretation:** For a natural brine above 2 mol kg⁻¹, choose a *specific* Pitzer database validated for its dominant ions, temperature, pressure, and candidate minerals. Within such a domain, the cited gypsum tests support errors of a few percent and other reported solubility tests support roughly 5–10%; outside it, even the **direction or phase** of an equilibrium prediction may become unreliable. Ionic strength alone cannot certify accuracy. (reiss2021gypsumprecipitationunder pages 3-5, møller1986predictionofbrine pages 1-4, mollerweare1982calculationofthe pages 1-5, reiss2021gypsumprecipitationunder pages 5-7, garcia2005measurementandmodelling pages 42-44)
