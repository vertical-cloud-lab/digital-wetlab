Question: In Pitzer and Mayorga (1973), how were the third virial coefficients (Cphi) determined, and for which electrolytes or below what molality or ionic strength were Cphi terms omitted?

Pitzer and Mayorga determined the third-virial coefficient, **\(C^{\phi}\)**, empirically—not from a separate theoretical calculation. They fitted it alongside \(\beta^{(0)}\) and \(\beta^{(1)}\) by least squares, primarily to the osmotic-coefficient values compiled by Robinson and Stokes. For selected systems, including hydroxides, hydrogen halides, and zinc halides, they instead used original cell-potential and/or isopiestic data. They sought agreement within experimental uncertainty over as wide a concentration range as feasible; a typical weighting gave equal weight through ionic strength \(I=4\), then weights proportional to \((4/I)^2\). (pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 1-7, pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 7-11, pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 11-15, pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 20-25)

The omissions depend on **which calculation** is meant:

| Context | When/where $C^{\phi}$ was omitted | Electrolytes or scope | Important qualification |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fitted parameters for inorganic 1–1 electrolytes | When the experimental data did **not reach 2 molal** | Table I examples with blank $C^{\phi}$: LiI, NaF, NaH₂AsO₄, KClO₃, KBrO₃, KH₂PO₄, KH₂AsO₄, KPF₆, CsNO₃, TlClO₄, and TlNO₃ | This was a data-coverage rule: dilute-range observations could not determine a reliable third virial coefficient. (pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 7-11, pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 41-46) |
| Borderline 1–1 case at 2 molal | $C^{\phi}$ was omitted after a three-parameter fit produced a “peculiar set of constants” | NH₄ClO₄ | KF also reached only 2 molal but yielded reasonable results **with** $C^{\phi}$ retained. (pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 7-11, pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 11-15, pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 41-46) |
| Selected hydroxide fits | The adopted parameter sets explicitly used no third virial coefficient | LiOH and CsOH | These were electrolyte-specific judgments based on the available cell data, not a universal concentration cutoff. (pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 15-20, pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 20-25, pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 41-46) |
| Type-B hydroxide mixed-cell analysis | Third-virial-related terms were ignored because only solutions **below 0.3 M** were used | Mixed MOH–MX galvanic-cell data | The 0.3 M limit applies to this particular cell-data analysis; it is **not** the general cutoff for omitting $C^{\phi}$ from all fitted electrolyte models. (pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 15-20) |
| Figure 7 approximation | $C^{\phi}$ was neglected in constructing approximate activity-coefficient curves | All 1–1 electrolytes represented by the approximation | This simplification was for rough graphical estimates, not the full tabulated fits. (pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 25-30) |
| Selected higher-valence table omissions | Blank $C^{\phi}$ entries occur where the available range did not support a useful third-virial parameter | Examples: Ba(OH)₂ and K₂SO₄ in Table VI; InCl₃ in Table VIII | These illustrate electrolyte-specific omissions; the paper does not state that the 2-molal rule for inorganic 1–1 salts is a universal rule for every charge type. (pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 50-52, pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. media 8b991a62, pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. media 161e9ce9) |


*Table: The table distinguishes omissions from fitted electrolyte models, the special low-concentration hydroxide-cell analysis, and the separate Figure 7 approximation. This prevents the 0.3 M analytical restriction from being mistaken for the paper’s general fitting rule.*

Thus, the paper’s explicit **2-molal criterion concerns whether the data extend far enough to fit \(C^{\phi}\) reliably for inorganic 1:1 salts**; it does not mean that \(C^{\phi}\) was dropped from every calculation below 2 molal. Separately, the **below-0.3 M** restriction concerns the analysis of type-B hydroxide mixed-cell measurements, where third-virial terms were ignored. NH₄ClO₄ is the stated exception to the 2-molal fitting rule: its three-parameter fit was unsatisfactory, whereas KF’s was acceptable. (pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 7-11, pitzerUnknownyearthermodynamicsofelectrolytes. pages 15-20)
