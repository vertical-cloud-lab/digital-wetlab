# PHREEQC Pitzer databases: Fe(II), Pb(II), and consistency

**The database name alone does not establish Fe–Pb coverage.** The bundled `pitzer.dat` contains Fe(II) but not Pb; Bozau’s *gebo* and the separately published `pit2010.dat` extend that starting point to lead. THEREDA is a different, versioned thermodynamic database that can export PHREEQC parameter files: its documented **2020 PHREEQC export** lacks Pb, whereas its **2021 lead selection** supplies a substantial Pb(II) model. These are distinct datasets, not interchangeable collections of missing parameters. (bozau2013hydrogeochemicalmodelingof pages 1-3, brendler2009theredathermodynamicreferencedatabase pages 5-7, tepavitcharova2014chemicalspeciationin pages 3-5, kunan2021thermodynamicandkinetic pages 5-7, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 20-22)

| Dataset or model | Fe(II) and Pb(II) treatment | Important qualification |
|---|---|---|
| **Bundled `pitzer.dat`** | Includes Fe²⁺ and Fe-related data; does **not** include Pb. Its core major-salt model descends from the Harvie–Weare Pitzer treatment, with Fe(II) among subsequently added, originally *largely untested* literature extensions. (plummer1988acomputerprogram pages 5-9, tepavitcharova2014chemicalspeciationin pages 3-5, kunan2021thermodynamicandkinetic pages 5-7) | Fe²⁺ being listed does not establish complete Fe redox, mineral, or mixed-brine coverage. Pb-bearing scale cannot be modeled from this file alone. (plummer1988acomputerprogram pages 5-9, kunan2021thermodynamicandkinetic pages 5-7, kunan2021thermodynamicandkinetic pages 8-10) |
| **Bozau–van Berk *gebo*** | An extension made by adding literature data to `pitzer.dat`. It explicitly adds Fe, Fe(+2), Fe(+3), and Pb master-species/redox definitions, along with reduced S and other components, for deep geothermal-brine and scale modeling. The application considers Fe released by casing corrosion and Pb-bearing phases including galena. (bozau2013hydrogeochemicalmodelingof pages 1-3, bozau2013hydrogeochemicalmodelingof pages 3-4) | The available 2013 application does **not** enumerate every Fe/Pb aqueous interaction coefficient or establish that all additions were jointly refitted with the original salt matrix. Its reported success at five wells is an application result, not proof of transferable internal consistency. (bozau2013hydrogeochemicalmodelingof pages 1-3, bozau2013hydrogeochemicalmodelingof pages 3-4) |
| **THEREDA PHREEQC exports** | THEREDA stores harmonized thermodynamic data and produces code-specific files, including PHREEQC files. A published comparison identifies a file named `THEREDA_2020_PHRQ`; its element-coverage table shows neither Fe nor Pb in that **particular export**. Separately, Moog documents the **2021 THEREDA Pb selection**, with Pb²⁺ and chloride, sulfate, hydroxide, and carbonate complexes, associated interaction parameters, and Pb solids. (brendler2009theredathermodynamicreferencedatabase pages 1-3, brendler2009theredathermodynamicreferencedatabase pages 5-7, kunan2021thermodynamicandkinetic pages 5-7, kunan2021thermodynamicandkinetic media b41d6180, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 6-8, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 20-22) | Do not infer the contents of one release or export from another. The documented Pb selection is valid at **298.15 K and atmospheric pressure**, not automatically at geothermal temperatures. The sources examined do not establish Fe(II) coverage for every later THEREDA export. (moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 1-2, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 20-22, kunan2021thermodynamicandkinetic media b41d6180) |
| **`pit2010.dat`** | Tepavitcharova and colleagues combined an extended aqueous-speciation database with `pitzer.dat` and added literature Pitzer interactions for trace metals, including Pb²⁺ with Cl⁻ and SO₄²⁻; Fe²⁺ remains in the underlying coverage, and Fe³⁺ was among their additions. (tepavitcharova2014chemicalspeciationin pages 1-3, tepavitcharova2014chemicalspeciationin pages 3-5) | Published calculations were at **25 °C** because temperature-dependent data were limited. Their application assumes oxidized waters and, for those calculations, neglects Fe²⁺: file coverage and the oxidation state actually modeled must not be confused. (tepavitcharova2014chemicalspeciationin pages 3-5) |
| **Other relevant sources** | The GEOTRACES/SCOR seawater Pitzer model documents Fe²⁺–Cl⁻ and Fe²⁺–SO₄²⁻ coefficients, Fe hydrolysis/carbonate constants, and Pb chloride, sulfate, hydroxide, and carbonate treatments. THEREDA has also received a consistently reassessed Si–Al Pitzer dataset. These are *parameter sources or specialized models*, not evidence that their entries can simply be pasted into another PHREEQC database. (turner2023sourcesofpitzer pages 3-6, turner2023sourcesofpitzer pages 6-9, turner2023sourcesofpitzer pages 9-13, turner2023sourcesofpitzer pages 21-26, miron2024si–alpitzerdataset pages 1-4) | PHREEQC files such as `llnl.dat`, Thermoddem, and the cited ThermoChimie exports may offer broader Fe/Pb species or minerals, but the published comparison describes LLNL and Thermoddem as **B-dot**, not Pitzer, activity-model databases. (kunan2021thermodynamicandkinetic pages 5-7, kunan2021thermodynamicandkinetic pages 8-10) |

**What is—and is not—in the THEREDA lead model.** Moog’s selected Pb model uses Pb²⁺ as its master species. Its chloride complexes include PbCl⁺, PbCl₂(aq), PbCl₃⁻, and PbCl₄²⁻; it also treats PbSO₄(aq), Pb(SO₄)₂²⁻, mononuclear hydroxo complexes through Pb(OH)₃⁻, and PbCO₃(aq) and Pb(CO₃)₂²⁻. Pitzer coefficients are *not* available for every included complex: the report specifically identifies PbOH⁺, Pb(OH)₂(aq), PbCO₃(aq), and Pb(CO₃)₂²⁻ as lacking experimentally derivable coefficients. Its candidate bicarbonate complex was not selected, and phosphate solids were included without explicit aqueous phosphate complexes. Thus “contains Pb” does not mean complete interaction coverage. (moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 2-4, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 4-6, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 6-8, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 20-22)

## Reported internal-consistency problems

1. **A direct mixed-parameter failure was demonstrated for a major-salt system.** In the USGS PHRQPITZ work, replacing the Harvie-model NaHCO₃ single-salt parameters with independently published Sarbar parameters changed the predicted solubility of nahcolite in Na₂CO₃ solutions. Adjusting *only* nahcolite’s equilibrium constant still gave poor agreement. Altering the coupled CO₃²⁻–HCO₃⁻–Na⁺ mixing interactions improved matters; a jointly revised set reproduced the measurements. The authors’ conclusion is that single-salt coefficients, mixed-electrolyte coefficients, and mineral equilibrium data must be consistent **as a set**. This is a demonstrated example, although it is **not** a published Fe–Pb-specific mixing experiment. (plummer1988acomputerprogram pages 13-16)

2. **Equilibrium constants inherit their activity-model assumptions.** THEREDA distinguishes consistency of thermodynamic data, consistency of algorithms and conventions, and consistency of *correlated fits*. It explicitly warns against combining standard-state constants extrapolated from conditional measurements with **SIT** parameters and constants extrapolated using **Pitzer** in one equilibrium calculation. Even two nominally Pitzer datasets can use different optimization equations or conventions, including the Debye–Hückel limiting slope. Parameters fitted against an assumed solid solubility product cannot safely be retained if that product is changed independently. THEREDA therefore groups linked data and distinguishes alternative brine and groundwater speciation sets. (brendler2009theredathermodynamicreferencedatabase pages 3-5, brendler2009theredathermodynamicreferencedatabase pages 5-7)

3. **Pb provides a concrete correlated-speciation example.** Hagemann’s Pb complex-formation constants, Pitzer coefficients, and mineral-solubility constants were fitted together using solubility, emf, and spectroscopic observations. THEREDA warns that changing those quantities independently—or adding or deleting a modeled Pb complex—can produce erroneous calculations. Source selections differ materially: its cumulative log₁₀K for PbCl₄²⁻ formation is **0.041**, versus approximately **1.33–1.40** in comparison selections tabulated by Moog; for PbCl₂(aq), the corresponding values are **2.225** versus **2.00–2.10**. Those numbers establish a consequential *difference in selected models*, not by themselves a measured failure of a particular hybrid PHREEQC file. (moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 2-4, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 4-6)

4. **Notation and species choices must travel with the coefficients.** The THEREDA Pb report converts Hagemann’s Pitzer *C* coefficients from the reported **Cγ** convention to its **Cφ** convention, and converts hydrolysis constants expressed using OH⁻ to reactions expressed using H₂O and H⁺. Copying the original numbers without their respective conversions would change the calculation. Independently, GEOTRACES distinguishes weaker Fe²⁺ chloride/sulfate interactions modeled with Pitzer coefficients from Pb complexes treated with formation constants *and* activity corrections; replacing one representation without revisiting its fitted constants risks an incoherent species model or counting an interaction twice. The latter is a methodological risk inferred from the documented alternative treatments, **not** a reported numerical diagnosis of *gebo*. (moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 2-4, turner2023sourcesofpitzer pages 3-6, turner2023sourcesofpitzer pages 9-13)

**Practical interpretation.** For a specified PHREEQC file and release, check Fe²⁺/Pb²⁺ master species, aqueous complexes, relevant binary and mixed-ion interactions, solid-phase constants, coefficient conventions, temperature functions, and validation against the intended brine. Prefer one evaluated salt-matrix model and import a trace-metal extension only together with its compatible speciation, interaction, and solubility data; where compatibility has not been demonstrated, re-evaluate the linked parameters and benchmark against independent measurements. In particular, neither the geothermal fit of *gebo* nor the existence of THEREDA’s 25 °C Pb dataset demonstrates that an arbitrary `pitzer.dat`–THEREDA–GEOTRACES splice is internally consistent. (bozau2013hydrogeochemicalmodelingof pages 3-4, brendler2009theredathermodynamicreferencedatabase pages 3-5, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 2-4, plummer1988acomputerprogram pages 13-16, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 20-22)
