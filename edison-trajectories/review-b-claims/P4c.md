Question: Is the 1925 Kendall and Sloan dataset on PbCl2 solubility in NaCl solutions considered reliable? Have later studies found that the solid phase in those experiments was not PbCl2 (for example a double salt or a basic lead chloride), and which later PbCl2-NaCl-H2O solubility studies exist?

## Assessment

**The available literature does not support rejecting Kendall and Sloan’s 1925 *NaCl* solubility data on the ground that their solid was not PbCl₂.** In a 1980 critical compilation, Clever and Johnston list Kendall and Sloan among sources of PbCl₂ solubility in aqueous NaCl without identifying their NaCl results as poor-quality data; they also include the authors’ pure-water value among measurements close to the recommended value. This is not proof that every NaCl point is accurate, but it is materially different from a documented phase misidentification. (clever1980thesolubilityof pages 13-14, clever1980thesolubilityof pages 11-13)

The most important qualification concerns **which chloride system is being criticized**. Moog’s 2022 THEREDA reassessment states that, at 298.15 K, the *PbCl₂–NaCl–H₂O* solubility diagram has only two solids—cotunnite (PbCl₂) and halite (NaCl)—and that the experimental data are well reproduced. Its Figure 4-1 includes Kendall–Sloan data in that comparison. The report’s explicit statement that Kendall–Sloan results are “systematically too low” occurs instead under *PbCl₂–MgCl₂–H₂O*; it also notes disagreement for their *CaCl₂* results. Those criticisms should **not** be transferred to their NaCl dataset. (moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor media 2fcc3a8b, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 17-20)

**No later study located here demonstrates that Kendall and Sloan’s NaCl-equilibrated solid was a sodium–lead double chloride or a basic lead chloride.** Double chlorides are identified in the *KCl* system, and a hydrated double chloride is identified in the *MgCl₂* system; neither finding establishes a corresponding NaCl phase. Basic lead chlorides, including Pb(OH)Cl (laurionite), are relevant under appropriate pH conditions, not evidence that one formed in the 1925 NaCl experiments. In a later NaCl-containing solubility study, Nriagu and Anderson reported that X-ray examination found **PbCl₂ as the only solid**. That directly supports the conventional phase assignment for their experiments, although it cannot retrospectively identify every 1925 sample. (clever1980thesolubilityof pages 13-14, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 6-8, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 9-11)

## Subsequent studies

The clearest independent successors include **Deacon (1927)**, who studied NaCl–PbCl₂–H₂O equilibria; **Mironov and co-workers (1964)**, whose value at 298.15 K in 3.0 molal NaCl appears in Clever and Johnston’s compilation; and **Nriagu and Anderson (1971)**, who measured solubility in 3.0 molal NaCl at 301.15, 313.15, 333.15 and 363.15 K and checked the solid by X-ray examination. Gromov (1940) also investigated complex formation in the named ternary system, but the accessible material does not establish whether it supplies an independent solubility dataset. (clever1980thesolubilityof pages 13-14, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 22-24, clever1980thesolubilityof pages 32-33)

Later work includes **Holdich and Lawson (1987)**, who measured lead-chloride solubility in concentrated NaCl solutions at 25 °C and at boiling temperature. Their results show a common-ion-driven decrease followed, at higher chloride concentration, by increased solubility associated with aqueous chloride complexes. **Tan, Bartels and Bedard (1987)** published *Lead chloride solubility and density data in binary aqueous solutions*, and **Zhang, Xu, Sang and Zhang (2018)** published measurements for NaCl–PbCl₂–H₂O at 373 K; their full experimental reports were not available for checking phase-characterization details. **Mgaidi, Fürst and Renon (1991)** is a Pitzer-model representation of chloride-solubility data, rather than an independently verified new phase experiment. **Vierling (1972)** studied mixed sodium *perchlorate/chloride* media, useful for comparison but not strictly the three-component NaCl–PbCl₂–H₂O system. (moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 22-24, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 25-28, vivar2025sustainablerecoveryof pages 2-4)

Lewin, Vance and Nelson’s 1953 note has a relevant title, but Clever and Johnston say that it **quotes PbCl₂ solubility from other sources**; it should therefore not automatically be counted as a new measurement series. The original 1925 article and several later primary reports could not be inspected in full here, so the conclusion is an assessment of the documented later evidence—not a claim that the 1925 solids were directly reanalysed. (clever1980thesolubilityof pages 13-14, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 22-24)
