## PbCl₂ solubility in water and chloride solutions

**Pure water, 25 °C:** The critically evaluated solubility of crystalline PbCl₂ is **0.03907 mol per kg of water**, equivalent to **0.03892 mol/L of saturated solution**, or approximately **10.8 g PbCl₂/L**. The molal and molar figures differ because they have different denominators; they are not conflicting measurements. These are *total dissolved lead* concentrations expressed as equivalent PbCl₂, not concentrations of free Pb²⁺. (clever1980thesolubilityof pages 11-13, clever1980thesolubilityof pages 13-14)

**Adding chloride at 25 °C does not produce a single, monotonic trend.** At first, added chloride suppresses dissolution through the common-ion effect. At higher salt concentrations, dissolved lead increases as lead–chloride complexes form and ion activities change. The following comparison distinguishes tabulated measurements from approximate readings of published plots or model curves. All salt concentrations and PbCl₂ solubilities are in **mol per kg of water**; a mole of CaCl₂ or MgCl₂ supplies approximately twice as much chloride as a mole of NaCl, before speciation. (moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 17-20, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 25-28, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor media f6ca07c6)

For **NaCl**, the 25 °C curve has a minimum of roughly **0.007 mol/kg PbCl₂** around **0.5–1 mol/kg added NaCl**; those are approximate graphical values, not tabulated measurements. A tabulated measurement gives **0.0171 mol/kg PbCl₂ at 3 mol/kg NaCl**, still below the pure-water value. By about **4 mol/kg NaCl**, the plotted value is approximately **0.04 mol/kg**, comparable to pure water. (moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor media f6ca07c6, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor media 93d7bf24, clever1980thesolubilityof pages 13-14)

For **CaCl₂**, measured solubility declines to **0.00513 mol/kg PbCl₂ at 0.548 mol/kg CaCl₂**—the lowest of the tabulated CaCl₂ measurements—and begins to rise by 0.807 mol/kg CaCl₂. A model curve indicates a much stronger increase at higher concentrations, but its agreement with measurements begins to deteriorate around **3 mol/kg CaCl₂**; values beyond that range should not be treated as reliable universal predictions. **MgCl₂** shows a similar decrease and subsequent rise, although at high MgCl₂ concentration a different, hydrated Pb–Mg chloride solid forms. **KCl** can also form lead–potassium chloride double salts, so chloride concentration alone does not determine saturation across different salts. (moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 25-28, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 17-20)

**Temperature:** In pure water, the reviewed solubility increases from **0.0239 mol/kg at 0 °C** to **0.03907 at 25 °C**, **0.0601 at 50 °C**, and **0.1272 at 100 °C**; the values away from 25 °C are classified as *tentative* in the evaluation. The increase also occurs in a chloride brine: at **3 mol/kg NaCl**, reported PbCl₂ solubilities are **0.0171 mol/kg at 25 °C**, approximately **0.0330 at 40 °C**, **0.0619 at 60 °C**, and **0.1449 at 90 °C**. The latter temperatures come from a different experimental series; they illustrate the trend rather than a universal temperature correction for every chloride concentration. The cited chloride-brine model itself is specified for 25 °C. (clever1980thesolubilityof pages 11-13, clever1980thesolubilityof pages 13-14, moog2022thermodynamicdatabasefor pages 20-22)

Finally, the frequently quoted **PbCl₂ solubility product, Ksp ≈ 1.70 × 10⁻⁵ at 25 °C**, is an *activity-based equilibrium constant*, **not** a solubility in mol/L. Computing brine solubility from Ksp alone without accounting for lead–chloride complexes and nonideal activities would therefore be misleading. (clever1980thesolubilityof pages 13-14, clever1980thesolubilityof pages 14-15)
