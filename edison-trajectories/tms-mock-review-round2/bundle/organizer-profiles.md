# Symposium organizer profiles (researched 2026-07-14, public sources)

These are the four organizers of "From Mine to Refinery: An EPD Symposium Honoring Shijie Wang." The mock
reviewers should be modeled on these specific people — their research emphases, industrial ties, and likely
priorities when programming this symposium — plus the honoree's community.

## Michael S. Moats — Missouri University of Science and Technology

Professor and Chair, Materials Science and Engineering; 20+ year career in extractive metallurgy focused on
non-ferrous **electrometallurgy and hydrometallurgy**. Industrial R&D background (ELTECH: coated titanium
anodes for copper electrowinning and other electrochemical processes). Signature contributions are
industrially adopted **empirical/predictive models for impurity control**: a predictive formula for bismuth
in electrorefined copper cathode (used by a large smelter to control anode chemistry) and an empirical model
for ferric reduction in copper electrowinning (used by a major copper company for benchmarking). Other work:
crushed-ore agglomeration for heap leaching, non-cyanide gold leaching, solvent extraction. AIME James
Douglas Gold Medalist. Likely priorities as reviewer: industrial relevance and validation, impurity
management in refining circuits, whether a model/dataset would actually be used by operators.

## Ramana G. Reddy — University of Alabama

ACIPCO Endowed Chair Professor, Metallurgical and Materials Engineering. Career centered on
**chemical/process thermodynamics**: thermodynamic modeling and assessment, molten salts, ionic liquids,
thermal energy storage media, electrochemical reduction thermodynamics (e.g., TiO₂→Ti in CaCl₂ melts).
Extensive editorial and TMS leadership history. Likely priorities as reviewer: rigor of the **thermodynamic
modeling claims** — whether the Pitzer parameter regression is thermodynamically sound, whether activity
models and data quality justify new parameters, and precise, conservative use of thermodynamic language.

## Michael L. Free — University of Utah

Professor of Metallurgical Engineering (Materials Science & Engineering), Associate Department Chair. Author
of the textbook **"Hydrometallurgy: Fundamentals and Applications"** (Wiley/TMS). Research spans
hydrometallurgy, electrometallurgy, and corrosion: copper extraction and related modeling, rare earth and
critical-materials extraction, electrowinning/electrorefining, **electrochemical process modeling**,
biooxidation, interfacial phenomena. Publicly describes leaching experiments where pH and flow are "monitored
and controlled by **automated equipment**" — i.e., he already runs automated solution-chemistry experiments.
Likely priorities as reviewer: solution-chemistry correctness (speciation, activity, pH at high ionic
strength), practical hydrometallurgical framing, and openness to lab automation if the chemistry is sound.

## Corby G. Anderson — Colorado School of Mines

Harrison Western Professor; Director of the Kroll Institute for Extractive Metallurgy; licensed professional
chemical engineer with **45+ years of industrial and academic experience** (operations, piloting, plant
commissioning, consulting). SME Milton E. Wadsworth Extractive Metallurgy Award (2009); ~16 international
patents. Expert in mineral processing, hydrometallurgy, waste minimization and recycling; industrially known
for **sulfide-system hydrometallurgy** (notably alkaline sulfide leaching chemistry for antimony and
precious-metals circuits) — i.e., of the four organizers, the one closest to metal-sulfide solution
chemistry. Likely priorities as reviewer: does the FeS/PbS work connect to real industrial sulfide
precipitation practice (impurity removal, effluent treatment, metal recovery), pilot-scale credibility, and
practical value of the data.

## Honoree context — Shijie Wang

Career industrial extractive metallurgist (ASARCO, Freeport-McMoRan, Rio Tinto Kennecott Utah Copper);
focus on extraction/refining of Cu, precious metals, PGM, Bi, Te, Ni, REE; former chair of the TMS
Hydrometallurgy and Electrometallurgy Committee. The symposium audience honors a **plant-side impurity
management and refining** community — abstracts that speak to operational impurity control, refining
chemistry, and process water will resonate; academic-methods-first framing will resonate less.
