## 2. Talk vs. poster

**Would it plausibly earn an oral slot?** Plausibly, but not strongly. This is not a clear reject and not a weak abstract. It is also not an obvious oral anchor for a symposium honoring an industrial refining career. The scope fit is real through impurity control, solution chemistry, thermodynamic modeling, process water, and Pb processing, but the listed Technical Focus Areas lean toward recovery/refining operations, not high-throughput FeS/PbS model-parameter discovery.

**Do the pros of requesting a talk outweigh the cons?** No, unless the authors are genuinely ready to write the required proceedings manuscript and can show credible March 2027 results beyond platform validation. The oral request would buy visibility, but it also creates a manuscript obligation and raises the standard: organizers will expect a stronger industrial/refining connection and enough data to justify proceedings publication.

**Recommendation:** Request **Poster**. One sentence: the abstract is likely acceptable as a poster because it fits the symposium through impurity management and hydrometallurgical solution chemistry, but it is too proposal-stage and too indirectly tied to the listed recovery/refining focus areas to justify taking on the oral-manuscript obligation.

**If the authors still request oral:** revise the abstract to name the Technical Focus Area connection explicitly: “Pb processing,” “impurity management,” and “process-water reuse,” and add one concrete phrase on “calibration checks” or “controlled sulfide chemistry” so the proceedings-paper promise does not look premature.

## 3. Specific line edits

### Title edits

Current title:

> Automated FeS and PbS Precipitation Mapping to Improve Pitzer Modeling of Concentrated Hydrometallurgical Brines

Problems:

- Starts with “Automated,” which makes the work sound like robotics rather than extractive metallurgy.
- “Pitzer Modeling” is important but too methods-first for Moats and Anderson.
- “Concentrated Hydrometallurgical Brines” is acceptable, but “process waters” is closer to plant-side impurity management.
- The title does not signal a Technical Focus Area.

Better title options:

1. **FeS/PbS Precipitation Boundaries for Impurity Management in Concentrated Hydrometallurgical Process Waters**
2. **Mapping FeS/PbS Precipitation for Pb Processing, Impurity Control, and Pitzer Modeling**
3. **Automated Solution-Chemistry Mapping of FeS/PbS Precipitation for Hydrometallurgical Impurity Control**

Best title for this panel: option 1. It leads with the metallurgical use case and still keeps the technical core.

### Abstract edits

Concrete changes I would make:

- Change “Iron and lead sulfide precipitation affects impurity control…” to **“Sulfide precipitation of iron and lead affects impurity management…”** because “impurity management” is the flyer’s Technical Focus Area language.
- Change “concentrated-brine thermodynamic models” to **“concentrated-solution models”** or **“process-water thermodynamic models”** to avoid a petroleum-brine feel.
- Keep PHREEQC/Pitzer, but do not put it before the plant relevance.
- Replace “Opentrons-based autonomous precipitation-mapping platform” with **“automated precipitation-mapping platform”**. The brand does not help these organizers. Put Opentrons on the poster if useful.
- Add **“high-ionic-strength pH measurement”** or **“calibration checks”** to reassure Reddy and Free.
- Replace “image-based detection” with **“image-based endpoint detection”**. This sounds less like crude visual classification and more like a defined measurement.
- Add **“relevant to Pb processing, sulfide precipitation control, and process-water reuse”** to make the Technical Focus Area connection explicit.
- Keep “rather than a completed parameter database.” That honesty is important and should remain.

Things individual organizers may dislike:

- **Moats:** too much platform/model language without a plant decision or impurity-management payoff.
- **Reddy:** any implication that boundary shifts alone are enough to regress a reliable Pitzer parameter set.
- **Free:** vague pH/speciation handling at 0.5–5 molal ionic strength.
- **Anderson:** no mention of sulfide oxidation/controlled sulfide chemistry and no explicit connection to industrial sulfide precipitation or Pb processing.

## 4. Revised version

**Title:** FeS/PbS Precipitation Boundaries for Impurity Management in Concentrated Hydrometallurgical Process Waters

**Abstract:** Sulfide precipitation of iron and lead affects impurity management, metal recovery, and process-water chemistry in hydrometallurgical circuits, but concentrated-solution models often lack Pitzer interactions for Fe2+–HS-, Pb2+–HS-, and metal–organic-acid complexes. We will present an automated precipitation-mapping platform with controlled temperature staging, high-ionic-strength pH measurement, and image-based FeS/PbS endpoint detection. Latin hypercube sampling and active learning will map precipitation boundaries across 0.5–5 molal ionic strength, pH 4–9, 25–90 °C, and acetate/propionate concentration. Boundary shifts will be compared with PHREEQC Pitzer predictions to identify parameter gaps relevant to Pb processing, sulfide precipitation control, and process-water reuse. Early results will emphasize reproducibility, calibration checks, and initial FeS/PbS boundary data rather than a completed parameter database. The approach provides practical solution-chemistry data and a low-cost template for automated hydrometallurgical experiments.

Word count: 130 by whitespace-style counting, safely under the 150-word limit.

## 5. Bottom line

The four-organizer panel would likely program this as a **poster**, not reject it and probably not prioritize it for oral. The abstract fits the symposium through impurity management, process-water chemistry, thermodynamic modeling, solution chemistry, and Pb processing, but it sits at the edge of the flyer’s seven listed Technical Focus Areas because FeS/PbS precipitation mapping is not itself a named recovery/refining topic. The single highest-impact remaining change is to move the framing from “automated Pitzer-model improvement” to “impurity management in hydrometallurgical process waters,” with explicit mention of Pb processing and sulfide precipitation control. The organizer-panel-specific red flag is not scientific novelty; it is programming fit. In an honorary symposium built around industrial extraction and refining, they will ask whether the talk/poster helps the refining audience, not whether the automation platform is clever.
