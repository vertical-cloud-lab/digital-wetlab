# Does the Bozau (2013) "gebo" Extended pitzer.dat Database Dramatically Affect the Significance, Novelty, and Impact of the Proposed Work?

## Short Answer: **No, it does not dramatically affect the proposal's significance, novelty, or impact.** The proposal's core gap remains intact, though the PI should acknowledge and discuss this database.

---

## What the AGRIS Record Refers To

The AGRIS record (65df64da6eef00c2cea28930) corresponds to:

**Bozau, E. (2013).** "Prozessmodellierung hochsalinarer Wässer mit einem erweiterten PHREEQC-Datensatz" [Extension of the PHREEQC database "pitzer.dat" for modeling hydrogeochemical processes in saline waters and brines]. *Grundwasser*, 18(2), 93–98. DOI: 10.1007/s00767-013-0222-8 (12 citations as of this analysis).

## What the "gebo" Database Adds

The Bozau (2013) "gebo" dataset extends the standard PHREEQC pitzer.dat by adding the following **solution master species**: Fe, Fe(+2), Fe(+3), S(−2), N, N(+5), N(+3), N(0), N(−3), C(−4), Si, Zn, Pb, and Al. For these species, associated aqueous complexes, solid phases, gases, equilibrium constants (with temperature dependence), and Pitzer parameters were added where available.

## Critical Finding: The Metal-Sulfide Pitzer Parameter Gap Persists

Based on the literature search results (confirmed by the Edison scientific literature query tool), **Bozau (2013) explicitly states that no literature Pitzer parameters were found for interactions of aqueous Pb and Zn species with major ions.** Pb/Zn aqueous complexes and thermodynamic data were therefore imported from **llnl.dat and phreeqc.dat** — i.e., they are NOT supported by dedicated Pb/Zn Pitzer interaction coefficients. This means:

1. **Pb²⁺–HS⁻ Pitzer parameters: Still absent.** The gebo dataset does NOT fill this gap.
2. **Pb²⁺ Pitzer binary parameters with any anion: Still absent** (thermodynamic data borrowed from non-Pitzer databases).
3. **Fe²⁺–HS⁻ Pitzer parameters: No evidence of inclusion.** While Fe(+2) is added as a master species (along with Fe²⁺–Cl⁻ interactions that may have been included from existing literature), the specific Fe²⁺–HS⁻ binary Pitzer parameters (β⁰, β¹, Cᶲ) are not documented as present.
4. **HS⁻ parameters with major cations:** The gebo dataset cites Millero (1986) for HS⁻ Pitzer parameters — these are the same Na⁺–HS⁻, K⁺–HS⁻, Mg²⁺–HS⁻, Ca²⁺–HS⁻ parameters already known in the literature and already acknowledged in the proposal.

## Impact Assessment on the Proposal

### Significance: **Minimally affected**
- The proposal's central claim — that Pitzer parameters for Fe²⁺–HS⁻ and Pb²⁺–HS⁻ are absent from standard databases — remains valid even considering the gebo extension.
- Bozau's own paper confirms that Pb/Zn could not be parameterized with Pitzer coefficients due to absence of literature data. This actually *reinforces* the proposal's gap claim.
- The gebo dataset is an "unofficial" extension with only 12 citations in 12+ years, suggesting limited community adoption.

### Novelty: **Not significantly reduced**
- The 34 target parameters identified in the proposal (particularly Fe²⁺–HS⁻ binary terms, Pb²⁺–HS⁻ binary terms, higher-order mixing parameters θ and ψ for these cations with Na⁺/Ca²⁺, and neutral-species interactions) remain unmeasured.
- The gebo dataset adds the *framework* (master species definitions) that would allow these calculations IF the parameters existed — but it does not provide the parameters themselves.
- The fact that Bozau attempted this extension and explicitly noted the absence of Pb/Zn Pitzer parameters validates the proposal's identification of this gap.

### Impact: **Slightly enhanced, if anything**
- The existence of the gebo framework means that the Pitzer parameters generated by the proposed work could be directly incorporated into an existing extended database structure, making the proposed parameters even more immediately usable.
- The gebo work demonstrates community demand for these extensions to pitzer.dat.

## What the PI Should Do

1. **Cite Bozau (2013)** in the proposal as additional evidence that the gap exists — Bozau attempted to extend pitzer.dat for Fe, Pb, and S(−2) but could not include metal–sulfide Pitzer parameters because they don't exist in the literature.
2. **Use the gebo database as a comparative baseline** alongside standard pitzer.dat and Wang (2024)'s ScaleSoftPitzer framework.
3. **Frame the proposed work as filling the specific gap that Bozau identified but could not resolve** — this strengthens, rather than weakens, the motivation.
4. **Note that Appelo (2015)** — another major Pitzer database extension (147 citations, Applied Geochemistry) — also does not include Fe²⁺, Pb²⁺, or metal-sulfide Pitzer parameters, as its scope is limited to the Na–K–Mg–Ca–Ba–Cl–CO₂–HCO₃–SO₄–H₄SiO₄ system. This further reinforces the gap.
